# Pain Points, Failures, and Unsolved Problems in Logistics and Scheduling Optimization (to 2026)

Scope note: about 18 search calls. Several primary sources (transportation.gov, ajc.com) were blocked by the egress proxy, so some facts come from secondary reporting of those primary documents. Where this applies, the entry says so.

## Q1: Documented scheduling/logistics failures and their algorithmic or system causes

### Takeaway
The biggest documented failures are almost never "the optimizer produced a bad answer." They are failures of **disruption recovery at scale**: crew-tracking and rescheduling systems that could not keep up with the volume of changes (Southwest 2022, Delta 2024). Other patterns recur: brittle single-point IT dependencies (CrowdStrike 2024, Blue Yonder ransomware 2024, NATS 2023), big-bang planning-system rollouts with poor fit to the business (Nike/i2 2001, Lidl/SAP 2018), and venture-funded freight-tech whose "matching" algorithms had no structural defensibility once the market turned (Convoy 2023, Flexport).

### Cited Findings

**Southwest Airlines, December 2022 holiday meltdown (airline crew recovery)**
- Nearly 17,000 flights were cancelled and more than 2 million travelers stranded during the December 2022 holidays. DOT settled for $140 million, which DOT described as 30x larger than any previous DOT penalty. Much of the sum goes to compensating future passengers. — [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/southwest-airlines-reaches-140m-settlement-dot-2022-holiday-debacle); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/southwest-airlines-140-million-settlement-feds-december-2022-debacle/)
- The penalty was announced on December 18, 2023. — [CNN Business](https://www.cnn.com/2023/12/18/business/southwest-fine-canceled-flights/index.html); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2023/12/18/1219906471/southwest-airlines-2022-meltdown-fined-faa); DOT primary release (blocked, not fetched): [DOT](https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/dot-penalizes-southwest-airlines-140-million-2022-holiday-meltdown)
- A winter storm started it, but Southwest kept struggling after other airlines recovered, partly because its crew-scheduling system was overloaded. Pilot and flight-attendant unions blamed "antiquated" scheduling technology. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2023/12/18/1219906471/southwest-airlines-2022-meltdown-fined-faa); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/southwest-airlines-140-million-settlement-feds-december-2022-debacle/)
- DOT's legal finding was about **consumer-protection failures** (inadequate customer service, stranded travelers), not the software itself. — [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/southwest-airlines-reaches-140m-settlement-dot-2022-holiday-debacle)
- Southwest said the meltdown cost more than $1.1 billion in refunds, reimbursements, extra expenses and lost revenue. — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/southwest-airlines-140-million-settlement-feds-december-2022-debacle/)
- The crew-recovery tool was SkySolver, a GE-originated system. Faced with a huge volume of crew-schedule changes, it could not match crew members to flights. Southwest had been working with GE on a new version. — [Benzinga (Jan 2023)](https://www.benzinga.com/news/23/01/30405897/southwest-airlines-hires-consultant-to-ward-off-future-holiday-anomalies)
- The CEO said the tech "generally worked as designed" and that the problem was volume. In the CEO's words from a NYT interview: "We just couldn't keep up with the volume." — [CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/southwest-airlines-ceo-technology-operations-modernization/641439/); [NYT interview republished via dnyuz](https://dnyuz.com/2023/01/13/we-just-couldnt-keep-up-with-the-volume/)
- Southwest hired Oliver Wyman for a "top to bottom" review. — [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/23/01/30405897/southwest-airlines-hires-consultant-to-ward-off-future-holiday-anomalies)
- Commentators pointed to poor integration between separate airline software systems (crew, ops, communications) as a structural cause. — [Slashdot summary](https://it.slashdot.org/story/23/01/03/1446244/southwest-meltdown-shows-airlines-need-tighter-software-integration)
- Newsweek later reported that Southwest "avoids $11M" of the penalty. This appears to be a DOT waiver or credit adjustment, but I did not verify the details. — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/southwest-airlines-avoids-11m-fine-for-2022-holiday-travel-meltdown-11170736); [Travel Noire](https://travelnoire.com/traveler-accountability-southwest-waiver)

**Delta Air Lines after CrowdStrike, July 19, 2024**
- A faulty CrowdStrike Falcon update crashed Windows machines worldwide on July 19, 2024. Over five days Delta cancelled about 7,000 flights and delayed more than 10,000. — [Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/delta-avoids-fines-dot-probe-2024-crowdstrike-chaos/); [Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Airline-News/DOT-ends-inquiry-of-CrowdStrike-crash-that-plagued-Delta)
- Delta's main bottleneck was its **crew-tracking system**, which could not process the volume of changes. Aircraft and passengers ended up in one place and crews in another. — [Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/delta-avoids-fines-dot-probe-2024-crowdstrike-chaos/)
- DOT (Secretary Buttigieg) opened an investigation within days. — [FOX 5 Atlanta](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/delta-flight-cancelations-crowdstrike-tech-outage-response)
- Delta sued CrowdStrike over the outage. — [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/news/business/delta-files-suit-against-crowdstrike-over-outage-that-preceded-meltdown/5EIVQQT7PBEGHHEXYVMULHI3GI/)
- The Trump-administration DOT closed the probe without major fines. DOT stated that "Delta's passengers received prompt refunds, adequate baggage assistance, and appropriate assistance for passengers with disabilities." Reporting frames the closure as part of broader scaled-back enforcement. — [Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Airline-News/DOT-ends-inquiry-of-CrowdStrike-crash-that-plagued-Delta); [The Beat](https://www.thebeat.travel/News/DOT-Ends-Delta-Investigation-Amid-Broader-Deregulatory-Agenda); [AJC, URL dated 2026/06](https://www.ajc.com/business/2026/06/dot-drops-investigation-of-delta-for-crowdstrike-outage/)

**Blue Yonder ransomware attack, November 21, 2024 (SaaS single-vendor dependency)**
- Blue Yonder's managed-services hosted environment was disrupted on Nov 21, 2024 by ransomware. The attack is attributed to the Termite gang, which claimed to have stolen 680 GB of data. — [TechInformed](https://techinformed.com/starbucks-and-uk-supermarkets-impacted-by-blue-yonder-attack/); [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/starbucks-grocery-stores-hit-by-blue-yonder-ransomware-attack/); [BlackFog](https://www.blackfog.com/ransomware-meets-retail-blue-yonder-attack/)
- **Starbucks:** back-end barista scheduling, time tracking and pay were disrupted across about 11,000 North American stores. Managers fell back to pen and paper. — [IT Pro](https://www.itpro.com/security/cyber-attacks/blue-yonder-ransomware-attack-disrupts-grocery-retail-and-hospitality-firms); [TechInformed](https://techinformed.com/starbucks-and-uk-supermarkets-impacted-by-blue-yonder-attack/)
- **Morrisons:** its warehouse management system for fresh food and produce was hit, and it ran on backup systems. Supplier deliveries and product availability were affected. **Sainsbury's** activated contingency plans and recovered faster. — [Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2024/security/ransomware-attack-on-blue-yonder-disrupts-starbucks-sainsburys-morrisons)
- Blue Yonder has more than 3,000 customers, including Tesco, Morrisons, Sainsbury's, ASDA, Starbucks, DHL, P&G and Nestlé. — [Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2024/security/ransomware-attack-on-blue-yonder-disrupts-starbucks-sainsburys-morrisons); [SupplyChains Magazine](https://supplychains.com/blue-yonder-cyberattack/)

**NATS UK air traffic control flight-plan failure, August 28, 2023**
- More than 700,000 passengers were affected: 300,000 by cancellations, 95,000 by delays over three hours, and 300,000 by shorter delays. — [UK CAA](https://www.caa.co.uk/newsroom/news/aviation-regulator-publishes-independent-review-into-august-2023-nats-flight-planning-system-failure/)
- The independent review (CAP2993) published its final report on Nov 14, 2024, with 34 recommendations: 12 to NATS, 11 to the CAA, 6 to airlines and airports, 5 to government. These cover contingency and engineering resource arrangements, earlier notification of disruption, and passenger support. — [UK CAA CAP2993](https://www.caa.co.uk/CAP2993); [CAA review page](https://www.caa.co.uk/commercial-industry/airspace/air-traffic-management-and-air-navigational-services/air-navigation-services/nats-august-2023-failure-review/)
- The widely reported technical trigger was a single flight plan with duplicate waypoint names that the automated system could not process. It then failed safe into manual processing. An independent technical analysis walks through this. — [James Haydon technical write-up](https://jameshaydon.github.io/nats-fail/) (secondary; I did not read the CAA's technical report CAP2993C in full: [CAP2993C](https://www.caa.co.uk/data-and-publications/publications/documents/content/cap2993c/))

**UK rail May 2018 timetable crisis (planning-process failure)**
- The May 2018 timetable change collapsed services on GTR (Thameslink) and Northern. Up to 310 planned Northern services a day did not run. — [Rail magazine on the Glaister/ORR report](https://www.railmagazine.com/news/glaister-report-allocates-widespread-blame-for-timetable-failures); [ORR](https://www.orr.gov.uk/search-news/orr-inquiry-concludes-passengers-let-down-rail-industry-failures)
- The Glaister (ORR) inquiry blamed Network Rail, GTR, Northern, DfT and ORR. It found that DfT's phased Thameslink introduction stretched Network Rail's timetabling capacity, and that the industry "failed to foresee" the combined risk. Network Rail's System Operator "did not take sufficient action" in autumn 2017. Late electrification (NW Electrification Programme) was made worse because Network Rail "wrongly believed it could make up the time." Contemporary reporting summed it up as "nobody in charge." — [Rail magazine](https://www.railmagazine.com/news/glaister-report-allocates-widespread-blame-for-timetable-failures); [Commons Transport Committee](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm201719/cmselect/cmtrans/1163/116304.htm); [Yorkshire Post via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/yorkshire-post/20180920/281530816935195)

**Supply-chain planning and ERP implementation failures**
- **Nike / i2 (2001):** a "big bang" rollout of i2 demand planning across the US and Europe, reportedly not tested at live volume. It over-ordered slow sellers and under-ordered hot products. Nike attributed about $100M in lost sales in one quarter to it, and the stock fell about 20% on the Feb 2001 disclosure. — [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/264637/enterprise-resource-planning-nike-rebounds-how-nike-recovered-from-its-supply-chain-disaster.html); [Panorama Consulting](https://www.panorama-consulting.com/nike-scm-failure/); [Henrico Dolfing](https://www.henricodolfing.ch/en/case-study-16-nikes-100-million-dollar-supply-chain-speed-bump/)
  - Total spend figures vary: one secondary source says $400M. Treat this as unverified. — [MeltingSpot](https://meltingspot.io/en/blog/nike-supply-chain-failure-100-million-dollar-inventory-glitch)
- **Lidl / SAP "eLWIS" (abandoned July 2018):** planned since 2011, about 1,000 staff plus hundreds of consultants, and costs of about €500M against an original ~€201M estimate. The core mismatch was that Lidl valued inventory at purchase prices while SAP's standard uses retail prices. This led to heavy customization, and Lidl reverted to its legacy system. — [Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/18243/lidl-cancels-sap-introduction-having-sunk-500-million-into-it); [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/252446965/Lidl-dumps-500m-SAP-project); [Henrico Dolfing](https://www.henricodolfing.ch/en/case-study-12-lidls-e500-million-sap-debacle/)

**Freight-tech failures and distress**
- **Convoy (shut down Oct 19, 2023):** the Seattle digital freight brokerage, previously valued at $3.8B, closed after failing to find a buyer. CEO Dan Lewis cited an "unprecedented freight market collapse" and monetary tightening. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/10/19/bezos-backed-freight-firm-convoy-shuts-down-read-ceo-memo-here.html); [Trucking Dive](https://www.truckingdive.com/news/digital-brokerage-convoy-shuttering-operations/697246/); [TT News](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/convoy-stops-orders-cuts-jobs)

Others point to blitzscaling before unit economics worked. These are opinion. — [Logistics Navigators](https://www.logisticsnavigators.com/casestudies/convoys-shutdown-and-the-limits-of-freighttech-hype); [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/convoy-shutdown-trucking-startup)
- **Flexport:** at least four layoff rounds since Dec 2022, including 20% in Oct 2023, about 15% in Jan 2024, and 2% in Oct 2024 after merging its forwarding and fulfillment teams. It missed its end-2024 profitability goal and moved the target to 2025. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/16/flexport-kicks-off-restructuring-with-layoffs/); [Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/flexport-layoffs-fulfillment-forwarding-shopify/728950/); [Built In (secondary)](https://builtin.com/company/flexport/faq/stability-growth)

**Gig and last-mile algorithmic management**
- Amazon Flex drivers are reportedly hired and deactivated by algorithm. Issues outside a driver's control, such as locked apartment complexes or flat tires, can lead to termination, and disputing it reportedly costs $200. — [AIAAIC repository (drawing on Bloomberg 2021)](https://www.aiaaic.org/aiaaic-repository/ai-algorithmic-and-automation-incidents/amazon-flex-algorithm-delivery-driver-firings); [IEN](https://www.ien.com/operations/news/21533352/amazon-reportedly-firing-drivers-with-algorithm)
- In October 2025, New Jersey's AG and Department of Labor sued Amazon for misclassifying Flex drivers as contractors. Separately, the NJ AG sued Amazon over anticompetitive practices affecting DSP drivers. — [NJ DOL press release (Oct 21, 2025)](https://www.nj.gov/labor/lwdhome/press/2025/20251021_Amazon.shtml); [NJ OAG](https://www.njoag.gov/ag-davenport-sues-amazon-over-anticompetitive-practices-affecting-dsp-delivery-drivers/)
- NELP's policy brief describes the Flex model as shifting cost and risk onto drivers. This is an advocacy source. — [NELP](https://www.nelp.org/new-brief-sheds-light-on-the-amazons-dangerous-flex-labor-model/)

### Inferences
- Southwest 2022 and Delta 2024 share the same failure mode. The **crew recovery and tracking layer**, which is a large, heavily constrained re-optimization with legality rules (FAA rest, duty limits, union rules), gets overwhelmed when the volume of disruptions is several times normal. It then loses sync with where crews actually are. The optimizer's quality in normal conditions was not the binding constraint. Throughput, data freshness (crew location), and manual fallback were.
- Regulators penalize the **consumer-facing outcome**, not the algorithm. Enforcement also depends on politics (Southwest fined $140M in 2023; Delta probe closed without a fine), so outside pressure to fix recovery tech is weak and inconsistent.
- Blue Yonder, CrowdStrike and NATS show **concentration risk**: a single vendor or single input can take down scheduling for thousands of firms or an entire national airspace. Resilience depends on manual or backup modes (Sainsbury's recovered faster than Morrisons).
- ERP and APS failures from 2001 to 2018 share a pattern: big-bang cutovers, over-customization to fit idiosyncratic business logic, and inadequate volume testing. These are organizational and integration failures, not solver failures.

### Gaps
- Oliver Wyman's findings on Southwest were not found publicly, and I did not locate Southwest's post-2022 crew-system replacement details (the WSJ report is paywalled: [TipRanks summary](https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/southwest-working-to-strengthen-crew-scheduling-system-wsj-reports)).
- I did not fetch the DOT primary documents because the domain was blocked. The Delta probe closure date is unconfirmed (see above).
- I did not verify the outcome of Delta v. CrowdStrike or the size of Delta's claimed losses. Figures of about $500M circulated in 2024, but I did not source them here.
- Not researched for lack of tool budget: EU rail (Deutsche Bahn punctuality), other 2025–2026 airline IROPS events, and last-mile startup collapses beyond Convoy (e.g., quick-commerce firms).

## Q2: Sectors with persistent pain

### Takeaway
The pain is sharpest where problems are large, dynamic and heavily constrained, and where margins or OR expertise are thin. That covers airline and rail disruption recovery, truckload freight (about one mile in six empty, and near-zero margins), healthcare staffing (burnout, fairness), and retail and grocery execution that depends on a few SaaS vendors.

### Cited Findings
- **Trucking and freight matching:** ATRI's 2025 report put empty miles at 16.7% in 2024. The 2026 ATRI study reports 16.5% deadhead in 2025, a record operating cost of $2.336/mile (up 3.4%; $1.854 excluding fuel), and an average truckload operating margin of about 0.4% in 2025. — [ATRI July 2025](https://truckingresearch.org/2025/07/new-atri-report-shows-trucking-profitability-severly-squeezed-by-high-costs-low-rates/); [ATRI July 2026](https://truckingresearch.org/2026/07/new-atri-report-details-accelerating-costs-and-low-profitability-despite-cuts/); [TT News](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/atri-trucking-costs-2025); [secondary summary of 2026 figures](https://go-eka.ai/article/2026-atri-study-trucking-costs-hit-a-record-2-336-a-mile/) (I did not fetch the ATRI 2026 PDF; the margin and deadhead figures come from the secondary summary.)
- **Freight-tech market cycle:** the freight recession after 2022 destroyed the thesis behind digital brokerages (Convoy) and forced repeated cuts at Flexport. — see Q1 citations: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/10/19/bezos-backed-freight-firm-convoy-shuts-down-read-ceo-memo-here.html), [Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/flexport-layoffs-fulfillment-forwarding-shopify/728950/)
- **Healthcare and nurse scheduling:** qualitative studies (2025) find nurses want AI shift-scheduling to be fair, transparent and respectful of work-life balance. They also find that fairness should be defined with staff locally rather than imposed. — [PMC: nurse perspectives on AI scheduling](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12406402/); [JMIR Formative Research 2025](https://formative.jmir.org/2025/1/e67747)
- A vendor-blog claim, citing an ICN 2024 report, says over 40% of nurses have severe burnout symptoms. The underlying causes named are staffing shortages and unpredictable schedules, which software alone cannot fix. This is low-quality and vendor-sourced. — [Guideflow blog](https://www.guideflow.com/blog/nurse-scheduling-software)
- **Retail and hourly workforce scheduling:** the Blue Yonder outage forced Starbucks' 11,000 stores to schedule and track hours by hand. — [IT Pro](https://www.itpro.com/security/cyber-attacks/blue-yonder-ransomware-attack-disrupts-grocery-retail-and-hospitality-firms)
- **Rail timetabling:** planning capacity (people and time) in timetable bodies is a binding constraint, as the 2018 Glaister findings show. — [Rail magazine](https://www.railmagazine.com/news/glaister-report-allocates-widespread-blame-for-timetable-failures)
- **Supply-chain capability:** a Gartner survey (Feb 2025) found only 29% of supply chain organizations have built the capabilities needed for future performance. — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-18-gartner-survey-shows-only-29-percent-of-supply-chain-organizations-have-built-necessary-capabilities-to-deliver-on-future-performance)

### Inferences
- Trucking's 16–17% empty miles have persisted even after a decade of load-matching apps. Fragmentation (many small carriers), backhaul geography and spot-market volatility limit what matching algorithms can achieve.
- In healthcare the constraint is socio-technical. The optimization problem itself (nurse rostering) is well studied, but adoption hinges on perceived fairness and on having enough staff.

### Gaps
- I found no strong 2024–2026 data on construction scheduling pain, high-mix job-shop manufacturing adoption, or tooling for small and mid-size shippers without OR staff. These were not searched because of the tool budget.
- There are no hard numbers on OR or optimization adoption rates among SMB shippers.

## Q3: Recurring technical pain points

### Takeaway
The recurring technical pain points are:
- **real-time re-optimization under extreme disruption volume** (scale and latency)
- **data quality and data freshness**
- **opaque algorithms that planners don't trust**
- **integration with legacy and heavily customized systems**
- **single-vendor and single-input fragility**
- **labor and fairness harms from algorithmic management**

A long-standing academic–practice gap persists in operations research and scheduling.

### Cited Findings
- **Scale and throughput during disruption:** Southwest's SkySolver "couldn't handle" the volume of crew changes, and Delta's crew tracker "unable to process the volume of changes quickly enough". — [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/23/01/30405897/southwest-airlines-hires-consultant-to-ward-off-future-holiday-anomalies); [Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/delta-avoids-fines-dot-probe-2024-crowdstrike-chaos/)
- **Brittleness to edge-case inputs:** in the NATS 2023 failure a single anomalous flight plan halted automated processing nationwide. — [James Haydon analysis](https://jameshaydon.github.io/nats-fail/); [UK CAA](https://www.caa.co.uk/CAP2993)
- **Data governance and trust:** Gartner (Sept 2024) named supply chain data governance and composite AI the highest-impact planning technology trends. It said accurate, complete data is necessary for end-user trust and adoption. — [Gartner press release, Sept 25, 2024](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-09-25-gartner-identifies-composite-ai-and-supply-chain-data-governance-as-highest-impact-supply-chain-planning-technology-trends)
- **Explainability:** Gartner (Nov 2025) says autonomous planning has passed the "Peak of Inflated Expectations" on its hype cycle. A lack of transparency about algorithms and heuristics, and a lack of trust in data output, is impeding autonomous planning at some organizations. — [Gartner press release, Nov 12, 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-11-12-gartner-says-autonomous-planning-has-passed-the-peak-of-inflated-expectations-on-supply-chain-planning-technology-hype-cycle)
- **Legacy integration and customization:** Lidl had to bend SAP to purchase-price inventory logic, which led to over-customization and €500M sunk. Nike's i2 went live big-bang without volume testing. — [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/252446965/Lidl-dumps-500m-SAP-project); [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/264637/enterprise-resource-planning-nike-rebounds-how-nike-recovered-from-its-supply-chain-disaster.html)
- **Siloed systems:** the Southwest meltdown prompted calls for tighter integration across airline software. — [Slashdot](https://it.slashdot.org/story/23/01/03/1446244/southwest-meltdown-shows-airlines-need-tighter-software-integration)
- **Cybersecurity and vendor concentration:** Blue Yonder (3,000+ customers) and CrowdStrike (a global Windows crash) show that a single upstream failure can take scheduling offline across sectors. — [Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2024/security/ransomware-attack-on-blue-yonder-disrupts-starbucks-sainsburys-morrisons); [Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/delta-avoids-fines-dot-probe-2024-crowdstrike-chaos/)
- **Labor and fairness:** algorithmic deactivation of Amazon Flex drivers, tightened delivery quotas, and misclassification litigation by New Jersey (Oct 2025). Nurses want a say in how scheduling algorithms define fairness. — [AIAAIC](https://www.aiaaic.org/aiaaic-repository/ai-algorithmic-and-automation-incidents/amazon-flex-algorithm-delivery-driver-firings); [NJ DOL](https://www.nj.gov/labor/lwdhome/press/2025/20251021_Amazon.shtml); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12157959/)
- **Academic–practice gap:** there is a long-standing literature on the gap between scheduling theory and practice. Classical scheduling theory fails to address the "total environment" in which scheduling happens. — [IJPR 1993 (McKay/Safayeni/Buzacott line of work)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00207549308956713). It continues in "Mind the gap between research and practice in operations management" — [IISE Transactions 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24725854.2022.2046893) — and in a 2026 INFORMS Journal on Applied Analytics piece calling the academic–practice gap a "strategic imperative" — [INFORMS JAA 2026](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.2026.0316). A 2025 INFORMS piece argues OR needs to become more accessible, learning from ML's rise. — [INFORMS J. Data Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/ijds.2025.0076)

### Inferences
- Failures cluster at the edges an academic benchmark leaves out: 5–10x normal change volume, stale state data (where is the crew?), malformed inputs, vendor outages, and human acceptance. Benchmarks measure solution quality on clean static instances, whereas incidents are about latency, robustness, graceful degradation and manual fallback.
- The "autonomous planning past peak hype" (Gartner 2025) combined with trust and transparency barriers suggests that explainable, human-in-the-loop optimization remains an unsolved product problem, not just an algorithmic one.
- Labor-facing scheduling (gig drivers, nurses, baristas) is increasingly legally and politically contested, which adds fairness constraints that most commercial optimizers do not model explicitly.
