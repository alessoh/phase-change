# Human, Organizational, Economic and Regulatory Barriers to Effective AI in Logistics and Supply Chains (2025-2026)

## Q1. What do 2024-2026 surveys say about AI adoption, pilot-to-production rates and ROI in supply chain and logistics?

### Takeaway
Across every major 2025-2026 survey the pattern is the same. AI use is nearly universal, but only a small share of firms run it at scale or can show measurable P&L impact. For AI in general, about 5% of firms capture value at scale (BCG, MIT NANDA). In supply chain specifically, 55% of CSCOs cannot say what return their AI spend is producing, even though AI now takes about two-thirds of digital budgets (Gartner, Aug 2026).

### Cited Findings
**Gartner (analyst; not vendor-sponsored)**
- Aug 5, 2026: 55% of chief supply chain officers are unclear on the ROI of their AI investments, even though 67% of supply chain digital investment now goes to AI. Sample: 394 supply chain professionals at organizations with at least $250M revenue (Nov 2025 to Feb 2026), plus 135 senior supply chain leaders (Jan to Apr 2026). Gartner's explanation is that use cases are multiplying faster than organizations can build change management to support them — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-05-gartner-survey-finds-majority-of-chief-supply-chain-officers-unclear-on-ai-investment-returns); [MMH coverage](https://www.mmh.com/article/gartner_cscos_unclear_on_ai_roi); [Consumer Goods Technology](https://consumergoods.com/most-chief-supply-chain-officers-unclear-ai-investment-returns-gartner-reports)
- The same Gartner release says project-by-project AI investment produces "franken-systems" that block scaling and lengthen payback. It says chasing short-term ROI leaves marginal long-term gains and fragile infrastructure, and it recommends "rightsized" change management tied to AI strategy and enterprise priorities — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-05-gartner-survey-finds-majority-of-chief-supply-chain-officers-unclear-on-ai-investment-returns)
- May 6, 2026: a Gartner survey release is titled "AI is Not Driving Supply Chain Operating Model Transformation". The body text could not be retrieved — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-06-gartner-survey-shows-ai-is-not-driving-supply-chain-operating-model-transformation)
- June 11, 2025: only 23% of supply chain organizations have a formal AI strategy — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/2025-06-11-gartner-survey-shows-just-23-percent-of-supply-chain-organizations-have-a-formal-ai-strategy)
- Feb 5, 2025: GenAI produces productivity gains for individual supply chain workers but creates "new complications" at the organizational level (title of the release) — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-05-gartner-survey-supply-chain-genai-productivity-gains-at-individual-level-while-creating-new-complications-for-organizations)
- Claim that "fewer than 30% of supply chain AI pilots transition to production", attributed to integration, data quality and governance issues. This comes from a trade-press article and a search summary. I could not confirm that Gartner is the primary source, so treat it as unverified — [SCMR](https://www.scmr.com/article/ai-in-the-supply-chain-from-pilot-programs-to-pl-impact)

**MIT NANDA, "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" (July/Aug 2025; academic, cross-industry)**
- 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots delivered no measurable P&L impact, despite an estimated $30-40B in enterprise spending. The method combined 52 executive interviews, a survey of 153 leaders, and analysis of about 300 public deployments. Workers at over 90% of firms use personal AI tools ("shadow AI") — [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jasonsnyder/2025/08/26/mit-finds-95-of-genai-pilots-fail-because-companies-avoid-friction/)
- Caveat: commentators call the evidence "preliminary and methodologically fragile" (small sample, and "no measurable return" is loosely defined). The report is not specific to logistics — [Medium critique](https://medium.com/@ai_93276/the-mit-95-of-genai-pilots-fail-report-what-it-gets-wrong-and-what-leaders-should-do-instead-3a6a1bd7a3d5)

**McKinsey, State of AI 2025 (Nov 2025; cross-industry)**
- 39% of organizations report any EBIT impact from AI. For most of them the impact is under 5%, and only about 5.5% attribute more than 5% of EBIT to AI. Nearly two-thirds have not begun scaling AI across the enterprise — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai); [CFO Brew](https://www.cfobrew.com/stories/ai-scaling-but-where-company-wide-earnings-impact)
- Supply chain management is among the functions most often cited for AI-driven cost reductions, alongside service operations and manufacturing — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)
- Workflow redesign correlates most strongly with EBIT impact. The early-2025 edition found only 21% of GenAI users had redesigned even some workflows — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai); [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@robi.tomar72/mckinseys-state-of-ai-2025-essential-insights-for-developers-leaders-f3c73f0a14c6)

**BCG, "The Widening AI Value Gap" (Sept 30, 2025; cross-industry, n > 1,250)**
- 60% of companies report little or no value from AI. 35% are scaling but not yet seeing significant value, and 5% are "future-built" and capture value at scale. Future-built firms get 5x the revenue gains and 3x the cost reductions, and their 3-year TSR is 3.6x higher. About 70% of AI value sits in core functions, including supply chains, pricing and factories — [BCG report PDF](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-Widening-AI-Value-Gap-Sept-2025.pdf); [BCG press](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings)

**MHI / Deloitte 2026 Annual Industry Report, "Rewiring the Future" (Apr 2026; industry association plus consultancy)**
- 28% of respondents use AI today and 54% plan to within five years. 24% call AI "transformational", and 48% rate its disruptive impact significant or higher (up 25 points since 2025). Barriers cited: unclear use cases, automation cost, limited understanding, difficulty building business cases, talent shortages and budget constraints. Leaders "don't yet understand how to apply AI to their operations," so they struggle to build a business case — [MMH](https://www.mmh.com/article/new_mhi_and_deloitte_report_finds_ai_biggest_disruptor_of_supply_chains_over_the_next_decade); [Yahoo Finance/BusinessWire](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/mhi-deloitte-report-finds-ai-171000075.html); [MHI](https://www.mhi.org/annual-industry-reports)

**Descartes / SAPIO Research transportation surveys (2025-2026) [VENDOR: Descartes sells logistics software]**
- Only 19% of shippers and 15% of LSPs use AI at scale. Barriers to scaling: data quality (45% of LSPs, 33% of shippers) and integration complexity (38% and 39%). 17% report fully automated transportation management. Sample: 600 senior transportation decision-makers — [Quiver/Descartes release](https://www.quiverquant.com/news/Descartes+Survey+Finds+Fewer+Than+1+in+5+Transportation+Organizations+Use+AI+at+Scale+as+Data+and+Integration+Challenges+Persist); [Descartes 10th annual study, Sept 16, 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/16/3363020/0/en/descartes-10th-annual-study-finds-transportation-technology-investment-has-increased-nearly-50-over-the-past-decade.html)
- An earlier Descartes survey headline: "96% of shippers using GenAI in transportation management" — [DC Velocity](https://www.dcvelocity.com/technology/descartes-transportation-management-benchmark-survey). Headline "use" and "use at scale" are very different figures, and vendor surveys foreground the first.

**KPMG (consultancy)**
- KPMG's 2026 article on supply chain AI strategy: most pilots do exactly what they were designed to do (sharper forecasts, better inventory, dynamic routing), but "the organization still doesn't trust the plan enough to execute it" — [KPMG 2026](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/supply-chain-ai-strategy-scale-beyond-pilots.html)

### Inferences
- Adoption is not the bottleneck. Every survey shows high trial or use and low use at scale (roughly 5-20%) or measurable financial return. The gap sits between pilot and production and between production and P&L.
- The numbers depend heavily on definitions: "use AI" (MHI 28%, Descartes 96% GenAI), "at scale" (15-19%), "value at scale" (5%). The report should not compare them directly.
- Most headline ROI statistics (MIT, McKinsey, BCG) are cross-industry. Supply-chain-specific evidence comes mainly from Gartner, MHI/Deloitte and vendor surveys.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm a primary Gartner source for "<30% of supply chain pilots reach production".
- The Deloitte and KPMG standalone 2025-2026 supply chain AI survey figures were not retrieved in detail.
- No reliable logistics-specific figure on SMB adoption versus large enterprises was found.

## Q2. Main barriers (skills, trust/explainability, change management, legacy systems, cost, SMB access) and evidence on human-AI collaboration in planning

### Takeaway
The most-cited barriers are data quality and integration with legacy systems, unclear business cases and ROI, missing skills, and weak change management. Behavioral OM research adds that planners treat AI forecasts differently from classical model forecasts. They penalize AI errors more heavily, and their overrides often lower accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- **Data and integration**: data quality is the top barrier to scaling (45% of LSPs, 33% of shippers), followed by integration complexity (38% and 39%) [VENDOR] — [Descartes via Quiver](https://www.quiverquant.com/news/Descartes+Survey+Finds+Fewer+Than+1+in+5+Transportation+Organizations+Use+AI+at+Scale+as+Data+and+Integration+Challenges+Persist)
- **Business case and understanding**: unclear use cases, cost of automation, difficulty building business cases, talent shortages and budget constraints (2026) — [MMH on MHI/Deloitte 2026](https://www.mmh.com/article/new_mhi_and_deloitte_report_finds_ai_biggest_disruptor_of_supply_chains_over_the_next_decade)
- **Fragmented architecture**: point-solution AI creates "franken-systems" that block scaling (Gartner, Aug 2026) — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-05-gartner-survey-finds-majority-of-chief-supply-chain-officers-unclear-on-ai-investment-returns)
- **Strategy gap**: only 23% of supply chain organizations have a formal AI strategy (June 2025) — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/2025-06-11-gartner-survey-shows-just-23-percent-of-supply-chain-organizations-have-a-formal-ai-strategy)
- **Skills and talent**: more than a third of supply chain hiring decision-makers had jobs they could not fill, mainly because of skills rather than pay (2026 outlook) — [Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/2026-hiring-outlook-improves-skills-ai-hurdles/809238/)
- Gartner, Feb 25, 2026: 55% of supply chain leaders expect agentic AI to reduce entry-level hiring needs (release title) — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-25-gartner-survey-shows-55-percent-of-supply-chain-leaders-expect-agentic-ai-to-reduce-entry-level-hiring-needs); [Procurement Magazine](https://procurementmag.com/news/gartner-agentic-ai-reshaping-supply-chain-roles). Inference: this may erode the apprenticeship pipeline through which planners build the judgment needed to supervise AI.
- A Gartner prediction, cited second-hand, says 60% of supply chain digital adoption efforts will fail by 2028 because of underinvestment in human judgment skills. A secondary claim says only 20% of US logistics firms have formal reskilling programs. Both come from a low-quality aggregator and should be treated as unverified — [ScopeRecruiting blog](https://www.scoperecruiting.com/blog/supply-chain-skills-ai); [WorldMetrics aggregator](https://worldmetrics.org/upskilling-and-reskilling-in-the-supply-chain-industry-statistics/)
- **Trust and explainability**: in KPMG's work, 35% of leaders see personal trust in GenAI as a major challenge. Where AI adoption is weak, leaders cite model explainability and training time as the main challenge. Organizations "don't trust the plan enough to execute it" — [KPMG 2025 trends](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2025/supply-chain-trends-2025.html); [KPMG 2026](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/supply-chain-ai-strategy-scale-beyond-pilots.html)
- **Change management**: Gartner (Aug 2026) says AI use cases multiply faster than organizations can develop change management — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-05-gartner-survey-finds-majority-of-chief-supply-chain-officers-unclear-on-ai-investment-returns)
- **Organizational learning and "friction"**: MIT NANDA attributes pilot failure mainly to tools that do not learn from, or fit into, workflows (a "learning gap"), not to model quality. It also reports that purchased or partnered solutions succeed more often than internal builds — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jasonsnyder/2025/08/26/mit-finds-95-of-genai-pilots-fail-because-companies-avoid-friction/); [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx)

**Human-AI collaboration and algorithm aversion (academic)**
- McKinley et al., "Is AI an Algorithm by Any Other Name? Behavioral Reactions to AI- and Model-Based Demand Planning Algorithms," *Journal of Operations Management* (2026). The study combines two lab experiments with a field study of about 575,000 forecasts, adjustments and sales records (91 SKUs, 485 stores) at an international retailer that switched from a model-based to an ensemble machine-learning forecast in 2019-2020. Managers do not treat AI forecasts like traditional model forecasts even when performance is identical. They react more strongly to AI errors and make larger adjustments when the AI performs poorly — [JOM/Wiley DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/joom.70045); [Walton College summary](https://walton.uark.edu/insights/posts/does_ai_change_how_demand_planners_adjust_forecasts.php)
- "Algorithm aversion during disruptions: The case of safety stock," *International Journal of Production Economics* (2024). It studies whether decision-makers abandon algorithmic safety-stock recommendations when disruption raises uncertainty — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0925527324002998)
- A theoretical paper, "Reputational Algorithm Aversion" (arXiv 2024), shows that workers override algorithms even when their own signal is less accurate, because following the algorithm hurts how others perceive their skill. Overall accuracy then falls below the first-best — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.15418)

### Inferences
- The binding constraints are organizational and data-related, not algorithmic. KPMG's "the pilot works but nobody executes the plan" and McKinley et al.'s heightened reaction to AI errors point to the same thing: failure happens at the human decision point.
- Relabelling a system as "AI" may itself raise override rates. That is an argument for rollouts that track override value-add, not just forecast accuracy.
- Reputational incentives, where planners are blamed for following a bad algorithm but not for their own bad calls, are a governance issue that KPIs can address.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 logistics-specific quantification of SMB access barriers (cost, data volume, IT staff) was found.
- The exact effect sizes (for example, accuracy lost through overrides) from McKinley et al. were not retrievable.
- The legacy-system share of failures has no single agreed statistic.

## Q3. How does regulation and labor relations affect logistics AI?

### Takeaway
In the EU, AI used for task allocation, monitoring and performance evaluation of workers (warehouse, driver and gig-courier management) is high-risk under Annex III point 4. Those obligations were postponed to 2 Dec 2027 by the Digital Omnibus. The Platform Work Directive adds human-oversight and explanation rights for platform workers from Dec 2026. In the US, state "robo-boss" bills have stalled (California SB 7 vetoed in Oct 2025, SB 947 reintroduced in 2026). Union contracts, above all the ILA's 2025-2030 master contract, set the hardest limits on port automation.

### Cited Findings
**EU AI Act**
- Annex III point 4 classifies as high-risk any AI used in employment and worker management: recruitment, promotion and termination, task allocation based on behavior or traits, and monitoring and evaluation of performance — [AI Act Service Desk, Annex III](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/annex-3); [artificialintelligenceact.eu Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/); [CDT brief on AI at work](https://cdt.org/insights/eu-ai-act-brief-pt-4-ai-at-work/)
- Deployer fines for breaching high-risk obligations run up to EUR 15M or 3% of global turnover — [artificialintelligenceact.eu staffing note](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/what-the-act-means-for-staffing-businesses/)
- Digital Omnibus on AI: provisional agreement on 6 May 2026, Council final approval on 29 June 2026, in force 27 July 2026. Obligations for standalone Annex III high-risk systems move from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027. AI embedded in Annex I regulated products (machinery, vehicles) moves to 2 Aug 2028 — [Council press release](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/29/artificial-intelligence-council-gives-final-green-light-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [CSA note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/)
- Relevance to logistics: annex point 4 covers task allocation and performance monitoring. Therefore warehouse pick-rate and workforce-management systems and driver-scoring tools are in scope. Pure route or inventory optimization that does not evaluate workers generally is not (inference from the annex text; the Commission's Annex III guidelines were still in draft) — [Modulos on draft guidelines](https://www.modulos.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-annex-iii-draft-guidelines-what-changed/)

**EU Platform Work Directive (Directive (EU) 2024/2831)**
- Adopted 14 Oct 2024, with a transposition deadline of 2 Dec 2026. Requirements: transparency about automated monitoring and decision systems, human oversight of significant decisions (suspension, account restriction, termination), and a right to explanation and to contest decisions. It also sets limits on data processing — [LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/guidance/the-eu-platform-work-directive); [ETUI](https://www.etui.org/publications/eu-platform-work-directive); [Mason Hayes Curran](https://www.mhc.ie/latest/insights/the-platform-work-directive)
- As of May 2026 no member state had it in force. Italy had a draft decree and Spain had opened a consultation (Apr 2026) — [Remote Work Europe News](https://remoteworkeurope.eu/news/2026/platform-work-directive-enforcement-divergence-2026/); [Ius Laboris tracker](https://iuslaboris.com/insights/eu-platform-work-directive-which-countries-have-implemented/)
- Relevance: couriers and last-mile delivery platforms that use algorithmic dispatch and deactivation.

**US state laws**
- California SB 7, the "No Robo Bosses Act", was vetoed on 13 Oct 2025. It would have required notice of automated decision systems and barred relying solely on them for discipline, termination or deactivation. Newsom said it failed to distinguish high-risk tools from innocuous scheduling and workflow software. SB 947 was reintroduced on 2 Feb 2026 to address the veto concerns — [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/news-insights/california-governor-vetoes-no-robo-bosses-act.html); [Crowell on SB 947](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/california-sb-947-no-robo-bosses-act-new-proposed-guardrails-on-use-of-automated-decision-systems-in-employer-discipline-and-termination-decisions); [SB 7 text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB7)
- Note: the veto reasoning ("scheduling, workflow management") covers exactly the kinds of tools logistics firms use, so the scope of any such law is decisive for the sector.

**Labor contracts**
- ILA-USMX master contract, signed 11 Mar 2025 and running to 30 Sept 2030. It bans fully automated terminals and fully automated equipment "devoid of human interaction". It also provides that "there shall be no implementation of semi-automated equipment or technology/automation until both parties agree to workforce protections and staffing levels." The contract followed the 1-3 Oct 2024 strike at 36 East and Gulf Coast ports, the first since 1977 — [Automotive Logistics](https://www.automotivelogistics.media/inbound-logistics/ila-and-usmx-sign-six-year-master-contract-securing-wage-increases-and-controls-on-automation/205446); [Logistics Management TA](https://www.logisticsmgmt.com/article/ila_and_usmx_come_to_terms_on_a_tentative_agreement_for_a_new_six_year_master_contract); [Supply Chain Dive on the automation impasse](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/ila-usmx-contract-negotiations-challenge-automation-concerns/732874/)
- The Teamsters-UPS national master agreement runs to 31 July 2028 and covers about 300,000 US employees (per UPS SEC filings). UPS reports cutting labor hours by nearly 10% by using AI and ML to shift volume to automated facilities. The Teamsters are pursuing limits on AI surveillance at UPS and Amazon, and the 2028 renewal is expected to put AI, routing and autonomous vehicles at the center — [UPS 10-Q 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1090727/000162828025049661/ups-20250930.htm); [Teamsters UPS 2023-2028](https://teamster.org/ups-ta-2023-2028/); [AIExposure analysis (secondary)](https://www.aiexposure.org/analysis/union-ai-bargaining); [TraxTech (secondary, trade blog)](https://www.traxtech.com/ai-in-supply-chain/ups-deploys-ai-automation-to-replace-43000-workers)

### Inferences
- Regulation mostly targets AI that manages workers, not AI that manages freight. Planning and optimization AI faces little direct regulation. Workforce-facing logistics AI (labor management systems, driver scoring, gig dispatch) faces the heaviest compliance burden from Dec 2026 to Dec 2027.
- The ILA clause requiring agreement before semi-automation turns technology deployment into a bargaining matter. Port AI ROI therefore depends on labor negotiation timelines (2030), not just technical readiness.
- The Omnibus delay gives firms time, but it also prolongs uncertainty, and the Annex III guidelines are not final.

### Gaps
- No verified primary text was retrieved for AI-specific clauses in the 2023-2028 UPS-Teamsters contract. The contract is known to include technology-change and surveillance provisions, but this was not confirmed in this session.
- New York and other state warehouse-quota laws (for example California AB 701, 2021) were not re-verified here.
- The ILWU (West Coast) agreement and the dates of European port labor disputes were not covered.

## Q4. What proposals exist to fix these barriers (human-in-the-loop, explainable optimization, reskilling, outcome-based pricing)?

### Takeaway
The main prescriptions are the same across sources: redesign workflows rather than bolt AI onto them, fund change management and judgment skills alongside tools, build shared data and platform foundations instead of point solutions, and design explainable, contestable human-in-the-loop decisions. Regulation increasingly requires the last of these. Outcome-based pricing is emerging but is still mostly outside supply chain software.

### Cited Findings
- **Workflow redesign** is the practice most correlated with EBIT impact from AI (McKinsey 2025) — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)
- **Reshape whole functions, not pilots**: BCG's future-built 5% reshape entire functions (including manufacturing and supply chain) and plan to spend more than twice as much as laggards — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/are-you-generating-value-from-ai-the-widening-gap)
- **Rightsized change management and a portfolio view**: Gartner (Aug 2026) recommends linking change execution to AI and supply chain strategy and avoiding project-by-project "franken-systems" — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-05-gartner-survey-finds-majority-of-chief-supply-chain-officers-unclear-on-ai-investment-returns)
- **Trust the plan**: KPMG (2026) argues scaling requires the organization to trust and execute AI-generated plans, which needs explainability and training — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/supply-chain-ai-strategy-scale-beyond-pilots.html); [KPMG 2025 trends](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2025/supply-chain-trends-2025.html)
- **Buy or partner and learning systems**: MIT NANDA finds external partnerships and tools that learn from workflow feedback cross the "divide" more often than internal builds — [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx)
- **Mandated human-in-the-loop**: the Platform Work Directive requires human review, explanation and contestability for significant automated decisions. The AI Act high-risk regime (from Dec 2027) requires human oversight and transparency for worker-management AI — [LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/guidance/the-eu-platform-work-directive); [CDT](https://cdt.org/insights/eu-ai-act-brief-pt-4-ai-at-work/)
- **Negotiated tech adoption**: the ILA model of no semi-automation until workforce protections and staffing levels are agreed is a template for co-determined deployment — [Automotive Logistics](https://www.automotivelogistics.media/inbound-logistics/ila-and-usmx-sign-six-year-master-contract-securing-wage-increases-and-controls-on-automation/205446)
- **Reskilling**: most sources point to internal reskilling over hiring, with new roles emerging such as "AI forecast coach" and "agent manager". The evidence is mostly from recruiter blogs, so quality is low — [ScopeRecruiting](https://www.scoperecruiting.com/blog/supply-chain-skills-ai); [SCMR on skills](https://www.scmr.com/article/ai-is-driving-change-in-supply-chain-skills-and-talent)
- **Outcome-based pricing**: Futurum's 1H 2026 buyer survey found 43% of buyers prefer consumption-based AI pricing and 27% outcome-based. Examples are mostly in customer service (Zendesk, Intercom Fin, Decagon). Deloitte published accounting guidance on outcome-based pricing for agentic AI in June 2026 — [Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/press-release/are-outcome-based-and-hybrid-ai-pricing-models-rewriting-the-vendor-playbook/); [Deloitte DART](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/publications/deloitte/industry/technology/accounting-outcome-based-pricing-agentic-ai)

### Inferences
- Outcome-based pricing directly addresses the ROI-measurement gap (Gartner's 55%), because payment is tied to measured results. It needs agreed baselines and attribution, the same capability CSCOs say they lack.
- Behavioral evidence (McKinley et al.) suggests some design levers: track the "forecast value added" of overrides, frame AI and model outputs consistently, and give planners structured override reasons. That is an inference; I found no trial in these sources that tests these interventions.
- Regulatory human-oversight requirements and good adoption practice point the same way. Explainable, contestable decisions help both compliance and planner trust.

### Gaps
- No documented case was found of a major supply chain planning vendor (o9, Blue Yonder, Kinaxis) using outcome-based pricing.
- I found no rigorous evaluation of reskilling programs in logistics that measures effect on AI adoption.
- Academic work on "explainable optimization" in supply chain planning (for example, interpretable solver output) was not covered in this session.
