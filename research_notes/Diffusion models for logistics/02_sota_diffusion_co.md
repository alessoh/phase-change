## Diffusion solvers win narrow niches, but classical solvers still run logistics

A diffusion solver learns the shape of good solutions from solved examples and generates a new one by denoising, which is exactly the mechanism behind this report's premise. It has been tested mostly on the Euclidean travelling salesman problem (TSP) and on graph problems such as maximum independent set (MIS), and only lightly on vehicle routing and scheduling. Results reported by a method's own authors are marked (self-reported), and figures seen only in search excerpts are marked (snippet). One Shanghai Jiao Tong University group built T2T, Fast T2T, COExpander, GenSCO and the ML4TSPBench and ML4CO-Bench-101 benchmarks, so we lean on independent checks by Xia and colleagues and by the UDC authors wherever possible.

### From DIFUSCO to consistency and flow models

DIFUSCO (Sun and Yang, NeurIPS 2023) treats a tour as a binary vector over edges, trains a graph network to denoise it, and decodes the resulting heatmap greedily, with 2-opt, or with Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) ([code](https://github.com/Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO)). Xia and colleagues reproduced it with MCTS at 0.51%, 1.18% and 2.77% on TSP-500, 1000 and 10000 ([ICML 2024 paper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v235/main/assets/xia24f/xia24f.pdf), Table 1), close to DIFUSCO's own 0.46% and 1.17% at 10.13 and 24.47 minutes per 128-instance test set ([awesome-ml4co transcription](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/awesome-ml4co/blob/master/data/benchmark_results.csv), self-reported). The UDC paper (NeurIPS 2024) instead relays "DIFUSCO+Search*" at 5.80% and 8.61%. Its appendix admits "We fail to implement all involved heatmap-based solvers" and says it used reported results, yet its figures match neither DIFUSCO's rows nor Xia's reproduction, so we treat them as an unexplained outlier ([UDC paper in the authors' repository](https://github.com/CIAM-Group/NCO_code/tree/main/single_objective/UDC-Large-scale-CO-master), Table 2 and Appendix G).

Later work attacks speed. T2T (NeurIPS 2023) adds objective-gradient search, and Fast T2T (NeurIPS 2024) replaces many denoising steps with a consistency model that jumps from any noise level to a solution ([repository](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/Fast-T2T)). Fast T2T's best settings reach 0.21% on TSP-500 and 0.42% on TSP-1000, but only after 6.9 and 18.3 minutes per 128 instances (awesome-ml4co transcription, self-reported). COExpander (ICML 2025) wraps a consistency predictor in an adaptive expansion loop and reports 0.25% and 0.64% in 0.66 and 2.43 seconds per instance on an NVIDIA H800 ([PMLR paper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/ma25r/ma25r.pdf), Table 3, self-reported). GenSCO (NeurIPS 2025) alternates local-search disruption with rectified-flow regeneration and claims 0.019% and 0.063% in 5 and 16 seconds for a whole 128-instance set, and a 141-fold speedup over LKH-3 to 0.000% on TSP-100 ([repository](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/GenSCO) and awesome-ml4co, self-reported). NEXCO (ICLR 2026) and M2GenCO (ICML 2026) report only relative gains in the abstracts we read ([Paper Copilot metadata](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists)). For DISCO, rejected at ICLR 2025 according to that metadata, and DEITSP (KDD 2025, [repository](https://github.com/DEITSP/DEITSP)), we found speedup claims but no absolute gaps or runtimes we could verify.

ML4CO-Bench-101 also lists 0.356% on TSPLIB with method, settings and time left blank, so that figure belongs to no named solver; the COExpander paper separately reports 0.367% on TSPLIB instances of 51 to 1,002 nodes (self-reported).

### The critiques

Xia and colleagues (ICML 2024, an oral according to their [repository](https://github.com/xyfffff/rethink_mcts_for_tsp)) replaced learned heatmaps with SoftDist, a softmax over distances with one temperature. Under default MCTS it scored 1.44%, 2.20% and 3.13%, behind reproduced DIFUSCO but ahead of the AttGCN, DIMES and UTSP heatmaps at every size where they ran, and it builds a test set's heatmaps in under 0.1 seconds against DIFUSCO's 3.61 to 28.51 minutes. Under the MCTS settings of Min and colleagues, TSP-500 gaps bunch together: DIFUSCO 0.90%, AttGCN 1.02%, SoftDist 1.03%, an all-zero heatmap 1.06%, UTSP 1.09% and DIMES 1.26% (Table 3). Given the same CPU time as the MCTS, LKH-3's gap is 2.39%, 7.78% and 33.82% of DIFUSCO's at the three sizes (Table 2), roughly 0.01%, 0.09% and 0.94%. Beyond the Heatmap, an ICLR 2026 poster according to the Paper Copilot metadata, finds that a parameter-free nearest-neighbour heatmap with tuned MCTS "can match or surpass" learned ones ([code](https://github.com/LOGO-CUHKSZ/beyond-heatmap-mcts-tsp)). FrontierCO, also an ICLR 2026 poster, tested 16 learned solvers on competition and library instances and found "a persistent performance gap that widens under structurally challenging and large instance sizes" ([repository](https://github.com/sunnweiwei/FrontierCO)).

### Graph problems and the economics of reuse

The clearest diffusion wins come on graph problems, and only against time-limited exact solvers. In ML4CO-Bench-101, COExpander beats KaMIS by 0.041% on ER-700-800 in 1.39 seconds against 60.75, stays within 0.026% of Gurobi on every vertex-cover set, yet trails KaMIS by 2.741% on RB-giant. UDC's relayed MIS table labels the stronger DIFUSCO and T2T settings "DIFUSCO-MCTS*" and "T2T-MCTS*" (8.36% and 7.80% below KaMIS), although DIFUSCO's published MIS scripts use greedy or sampling decoding, so the label looks mistaken. The unsupervised DiffUCO (ICML 2024), which learns from the objective without solved examples, is weaker: 9.9% below KaMIS on RB-large MIS, 2.7% worse than Gurobi on BA-large dominating set and 14.4% worse on RB-small maximum clique. Its one win is a Max-Cut 0.11% above Gurobi on BA-large in 53 seconds, against 41.6 hours of Gurobi time (Tables 1 and 3). We found no verifiable gaps for SDDS (ICLR 2025, [repository](https://github.com/ml-jku/DIffUCO)) or CAM (ICML 2026, [repository](https://github.com/Shengyu-Feng/CAM)).

Whether training pays depends on reuse. A rebuttal script in the DiffUCO repository divides training time by the time saved per graph against Gurobi ([Rebuttal_plot.py](https://github.com/ml-jku/DIffUCO/blob/main/playground/Rebuttal_plot.py)). Run as written, it gives break-even points of 197 queries for large Max-Cut after 16.4 training hours, 482 for small Max-Cut, 48,216 for large MIS after about 5.6 days of training, 348,830 for small dominating set and about 1.75 million for small maximum clique. It ignores solution quality, so it flatters the model wherever the model loses.

### Routing, scheduling and diffusion inside search

On vehicle routing the verified evidence runs against diffusion. COExpander's authors report CVRP gaps of 3.90% to 5.34% against HGS, worse than a reinforcement-learning constructor with the same local search (1.91% to 4.17%), and concede it "performs less favorably" (Table 6). PyVRP 0.14.0 averages 0.31% on the X set and 0.65% on 1,000-customer time-window instances, while OR-Tools Routing, run once under the same protocol, averages 4.42% and 9.38%. We found no diffusion result on Solomon or Gehring-Homberger instances. For scheduling we saw only a paper pairing a diffusion prior with reinforcement learning for hybrid flow shops ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417426027272), snippet) and a rejected ICLR 2026 submission covering flexible job shops. Learned scheduling of any kind still trails: on Taillard instances L2D ([repository](https://github.com/zcaicaros/L2D)) averages 25.5% greedy and 20.5% with 128 samples, and the best self-labelling pointer network 7.4%, against 1.58% for CP-SAT.

The more promising pattern places diffusion inside classical search. GenSCO already uses generation as a search operator. DDEA seeds an evolutionary algorithm with DIFUSCO samples and recombines with a diffusion operator, reporting MIS sets 7.5% larger than Gurobi's at equal time on ER-700-800 ([repository](https://github.com/jsalvasoler/difusco_ddea), self-reported). Generative Large Neighborhood Search (ICML 2026) lets diffusion trajectories choose neighbourhoods for large set-cover problems and claims parity with MIP solvers at equal wall-clock time, but its abstract gives no numbers. We could not verify a DIFUSCO-LNS paper for integer programs or find a diffusion destroy-and-repair operator benchmarked on vehicle routing.

### Scale, training data and distribution shift

Because an edge heatmap has n squared entries, DIFUSCO keeps only 50 or 100 neighbour edges per city beyond TSP-100 ([reproducing scripts](https://github.com/Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO/blob/main/reproducing_scripts.md)), and Fast T2T's 100,000-city script reuses the 10,000-city model without 2-opt and reports no result ([script](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/Fast-T2T/blob/main/scripts/eval_tsp100000.sh)). FrontierCO reports that growing from 1,002 to 18,512 nodes costs LKH-3 about five extra seconds and DIFUSCO about six extra minutes ([arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16952v3), snippet).

Labels are also unlike a firm's history. DIFUSCO's README builds 128,000 TSP-500, 64,000 TSP-1000 and 6,400 TSP-10000 training instances by calling generate_data.py, a script absent from the repository ([data README](https://github.com/Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO/blob/main/data/README.md)); the generate_tsp_data.py that does exist defaults to LKH-3 with 1,000 trials and ten runs, so we infer, unconfirmed, that the labels are LKH-3 tours. The withdrawn GuideCO submission to ICLR 2025 reports "an exponential scaling law between the optimality gap and the amount of training data" (self-reported), a warning for sparse company archives. Shift hurts even on synthetic data: COExpander's 0.257% on uniform TSP-500 becomes 0.774% on Gaussian and 1.998% on clustered instances, which Concorde solves exactly in 5.07 seconds ([ML4CO-Bench-101 Table 9](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/ML4CO-Bench-101/blob/main/assets/performance_cross.png)).

### Where diffusion wins, ties and loses

Diffusion wins on Max-Cut and some MIS families, where it beats Gurobi or KaMIS under time limits in seconds. It ties on vertex cover, and on TSP-500 and TSP-1000 it draws level only with the Python LKH package or Concorde's proof times. It loses at TSP-50 and TSP-100, where GA-EAX and LKH-3 reach 0.000% in 0.22 seconds or less and the best learned entries are not diffusion models. At TSP-500 and TSP-1000 every verified diffusion gap trails a well-configured LKH at 0.002% and 0.005% in 1.85 and 4.64 seconds; GenSCO's five-second batch is the one possible speed win, unchecked for batching or hardware. It loses capacitated routing by 3.9 to 5.3 points to HGS and has no time-window or job-shop results to set against PyVRP or CP-SAT. For this report's thesis, a diffusion model trained on a firm's plans is best used as a fast source of proposals and neighbourhoods inside a classical solver, not as its replacement.

### Evidence gaps

The web-search budget ran out during this revision, and arXiv, OpenReview and the NeurIPS proceedings were blocked, so the section rests on GitHub-hosted PDFs (Xia, DiffUCO, COExpander, UDC), repository files, Paper Copilot metadata and the Thinklab awesome-ml4co transcription. We did not open the DIFUSCO, T2T, Fast T2T, GenSCO, SDDS, DISCO, DEITSP, NEXCO or FrontierCO papers, and found no absolute numbers for SDDS, DISCO, DEITSP or CAM. GenSCO's time basis and hardware, the source of UDC's DIFUSCO figures, the hardware behind ML4TSPBench and ML4CO-Bench-101, the CP-SAT limit in the Self-Labeling spreadsheet and any DIFUSCO-LNS paper remain unverified. Apart from Xia and colleagues, we found no independent reproduction of a diffusion solver against LKH-3.
