## Diffusion solvers now reach near-optimal Euclidean tours in seconds, but a correctly configured LKH-3 or HGS still beats them on routing, and on scheduling they have not yet been tested

A diffusion solver learns what good solutions look like from solved examples and builds a new one by denoising random noise step by step. That is the mechanism behind this report's premise that a model trained on past successes will "know what to expect". As of September 2026 the idea has been tested mainly on the Euclidean travelling salesman problem (TSP) and on graph problems such as maximum independent set (MIS). Vehicle routing has seen little testing and scheduling none that we could find. Results reported by a method's own authors are marked (self-reported), and figures we saw only in search excerpts are marked (snippet).

One caution shapes everything below. Much of the supervised line comes from a single laboratory, Junchi Yan's Thinklab at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, where Yang Li and Junchi Yan co-author [T2T](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/T2TCO), [Fast T2T](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/352b13f01566ae34affacc60e98c16af-Paper-Conference.pdf), [COExpander](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/ma25r/ma25r.pdf), [GenSCO](https://openreview.net/forum?id=9JM03CQwzC), [UniCO](https://openreview.net/forum?id=yEwakMNIex), [StruDiCO](https://openreview.net/forum?id=P69X3V4WwH) and the [ML4CO-Bench-101](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ye4ntB1Kzi) benchmark. When COExpander or ML4CO-Bench-101 re-measures T2T or Fast T2T, that is same-group evidence, not independent replication. We treat as independent only the studies by Xia and colleagues, Beyond the Heatmap and FrontierCO, plus COExpander's measurements of DIFUSCO. FrontierCO shares an author, Yiming Yang, with [DIFUSCO](https://github.com/Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO) ([listing](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BVprkacwFY)), though its findings cut against diffusion.

### From DIFUSCO to consistency models

DIFUSCO (Sun and Yang, NeurIPS 2023) treats a tour as a binary vector over edges. A graph network denoises that vector into an edge "heatmap", which is then decoded greedily, with 2-opt, or with Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS). Its paper reports 0.46%, 1.17% and 2.58% on TSP-500, 1000 and 10000 with MCTS, and 1.49%, 1.90% and 3.10% with greedy decoding plus 2-opt ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08224), Table 2, self-reported). The reproductions are close. COExpander's authors, who are independent of DIFUSCO, measured 1.59%, 1.83% and 3.07% with one sample plus 2-opt (Tables 3 and 22). Xia and colleagues retrained it and obtained 0.51%, 1.18% and 2.77% with MCTS, adding a caveat about "potential overestimation of reported gaps due to issues found in their code" ([Xia et al., ICML 2024](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v235/main/assets/xia24f/xia24f.pdf), Table 1).

T2T (NeurIPS 2023) adds a test-time search that "disrupts the local structure of the given solution by introducing noise and reconstructs a lower-cost solution guided by the optimization objective" ([README](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/T2TCO)). Fast T2T (NeurIPS 2024) replaces most denoising steps with a consistency model. In Fast T2T's own table, with four samples and 2-opt, T2T scores 0.48% and 1.07% on TSP-500 and 1000 against 0.87% and 1.31% for 100-step DIFUSCO under the same decoding, and Fast T2T's best setting scores 0.21% and 0.42% (Table 2, self-reported). The same group's COExpander re-run of Fast T2T with four samples and 2-opt gives 0.50% and 0.74% in 0.47 and 1.93 seconds per instance (Table 3, same-group). A single step with no search gives 7.50% on TSP-500 (Table 7).

The newest solvers turn generation into a repeated search step. COExpander, which wraps a consistency model in an adaptive expansion loop, reports 0.25% and 0.64% on TSP-500 and 1000 in 0.66 and 2.43 seconds per instance (Table 3, self-reported). GenSCO alternates local-search disruption with rectified-flow regeneration. It claims a 141x speedup over LKH3 to reach 0.000% on TSP-100 and about 10x to reach 0.02% on TSP-500 ([README](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/GenSCO), self-reported). StruDiCO (NeurIPS 2025) uses an absorbing noise that removes variables one by one, then refines solutions without gradients. It claims up to 3.5 times faster inference, 70% less GPU memory and up to 37.7% lower gaps than earlier diffusion solvers on TSP and MIS ([abstract](https://openreview.net/forum?id=P69X3V4WwH), self-reported). DEITSP (KDD 2025) alternates noising and denoising in a one-step model. It reports 0.10% on TSP-100. A model trained on TSP-100 and applied without retraining reaches 2.15% and 3.68% on TSP-500 and 1000 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13767), snippet, self-reported).

Other recent papers change the training objective instead. CADO fine-tunes DIFUSCO with reinforcement learning on tour cost and reports 0.88% against DIFUSCO's 1.86% on TSP-1000 ([arXiv 2602.08210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08210), snippet, self-reported). Combinatorial Adjoint Matching (ICML 2026) trains a diffusion solver with no solved examples and was also run on CVRP ([code](https://github.com/Shengyu-Feng/CAM)). We could not obtain its absolute gaps. Two claims deserve particular caution. DISCO says it denoises up to 5.28 times faster than other diffusion solvers, but its ICLR 2025 submission was rejected ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=6JDpWJrjyK)). EDISCO claims 0.08%, 0.22% and 1.20% on TSP-500, 1000 and 10000, but its ICLR 2026 submission was desk-rejected ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=A1JfGuXJvM)).

Asymmetric distances, the realistic case on road networks, are much less studied. UniCO (ICLR 2025) reduces problems to matrix-encoded general TSP and introduces MatDIFFNet, a diffusion solver for such matrices ([README](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/UniCO), self-reported). On asymmetric TSP, COExpander reaches 0.17% at 50 nodes but 1.57% at 500 nodes in 6.60 seconds, while LKH with 1,000 trials defines the 0% reference in 4.38 seconds (Table 3, self-reported).

The table hides a baseline trap. COExpander's appendix reports that the Python `lkh` package, "widely used" by DIFUSCO, T2T and Fast T2T, sets LKH's SPECIAL parameter, which "disables the use of both 2-opt and 3-opt" (Appendix D.2). Removing it cuts LKH-3's TSP-500 gap at 500 trials from 0.875% to 0.002% (Table 11). We confirmed that version 1.1.1 of the [package](https://pypi.org/project/lkh/1.1.1/) writes SPECIAL into every parameter file and that Fast T2T's [environment file](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/Fast-T2T/blob/main/environment.yml) pins that version. The repository does not include the script behind Fast T2T's claim that it "surpasses LKH3 in the early solving stage", so we cannot confirm which configuration that curve used. If it used the default wrapper, as seems likely, the comparison was made against a handicapped LKH. Against the corrected LKH-3 at 0.005% on TSP-1000 in 4.64 seconds, no independently measured diffusion configuration comes close.

### The critiques

Xia and colleagues' ICML 2024 position paper is the sharpest challenge. Their SoftDist heatmap, a softmax over pairwise distances with one temperature parameter, reached 1.44%, 2.20% and 3.13% under default MCTS (Table 1). They also define a Score, LKH-3's gap divided by the MCTS gap at equal CPU time, where anything below 100% means LKH-3 is more efficient. DIFUSCO scores 2.39%, 7.78% and 33.82% (Table 2). At TSP-500, LKH-3's gap was therefore about one fortieth of DIFUSCO-plus-MCTS's gap. Their Table 3 is the strongest evidence. Under the MCTS settings of UTSP (Min et al., 2023), a heatmap of all zeros scores 1.06% and 1.85% on TSP-500 and 1000, against 0.90% and 1.56% for DIFUSCO. A network trained on eight V100 GPUs buys less than 0.3 points over no information; the search does most of the work. Beyond the Heatmap (ICLR 2026), also independent, reports that a parameter-free k-nearest-neighbour heatmap with tuned MCTS scores 0.50%, 0.85% and 2.14% ([paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/e3bf2f0f10774c474de22a12cb060e2c-Paper-Conference.pdf), snippet). These beat Xia's DIFUSCO figures of 0.51%, 1.18% and 2.77%, but those used default MCTS, and we could not see Beyond the Heatmap's own DIFUSCO row under tuned MCTS, so the comparison is not like for like. A comment by Yimeng Min contests Xia's timing accounting but, as excerpted, not the all-zeros result ([arXiv 2406.09441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09441), snippet).

FrontierCO (ICLR 2026) evaluated 16 learned solvers against classical ones across eight problems, including routing and a flexible job-shop task. It found "a persistent performance gap that widens under structurally challenging and large instance sizes" ([abstract](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BVprkacwFY)). Its repository uses LKH-3 for TSP, HGS for CVRP and CP Optimizer for the flexible job shop ([data READMEs](https://github.com/sunnweiwei/FrontierCO/tree/main/data)). Apart from the DIFUSCO ablation in the table, we could not obtain which diffusion solvers it ran or any hard-TSP, CVRP or scheduling figures for them.

Scaling and distribution shift are structural limits. An edge heatmap has n² entries, so DIFUSCO keeps only 50 or 100 candidate edges per city on large instances ([scripts](https://github.com/Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO/blob/main/reproducing_scripts.md)). Across methods, gaps grow from roughly 0.1% to 0.3% at 100 cities to about 0.4% to 1.8% at 1,000 and 1.5% to 3.1% at 10,000. The best peer-reviewed diffusion result at 10,000 cities is the same group's 1.45%, reached in about a tenth of LKH-3's time (table). ML4CO-Bench-101 allows a like-for-like check of distribution shift for the same solver at each size ([Table 8](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/ML4CO-Bench-101/blob/main/assets/performance.png), [Table 9](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/ML4CO-Bench-101/blob/main/assets/performance_cross.png), same group). For its supervised graph network with MCTS, which is not a diffusion model, gaps are 0.001%, 0.001% and 0.005% at 50 cities on uniform, clustered and Gaussian points, and 0.005%, 0.011% and 0.037% at 100. For its consistency-based AE-Gen-SL solver at 500 cities, they are 0.257%, 1.998% and 0.774%. Because the solver changes between sizes, this does not show that shift hurts more at larger sizes, but clustered cities, which resemble real towns, cost the generative solver nearly eight times its uniform gap at 500.

### Unsupervised diffusion and graph problems

DiffUCO (ICML 2024) and SDDS (ICLR 2025), from Sanokowski and colleagues, learn from the objective alone, without solved examples. On MIS over RB-large graphs, DiffUCO's conditional-expectation variants (CE and CE-ST8) both find sets of 38.87 against KaMIS's 43.15 and Gurobi's 40.90. CE-ST8 takes 1 minute 5 seconds and CE 4 minutes 57 seconds for the test set, against about two hours for the classical solvers (Table 1, self-reported). On Max-Cut over BA-large graphs, plain DiffUCO scores 2947.27 in 4 seconds, and CE-ST8 and CE reach 2947.53 in 53 seconds and 3 minutes 47 seconds. Gurobi, capped at 300 seconds per graph, scores 2944.38 (Table 3, self-reported). SDDS reports state-of-the-art unsupervised results ([abstract](https://openreview.net/forum?id=peNgxpbdxB), self-reported), but we could not obtain its objective values against KaMIS or Gurobi.

COExpander reports further wins over Gurobi given 3,600 seconds. It scores 48.43 against 44.81 on ER-1400-1600 MIS, 84.12 against 81.52 on RB-GIANT Max-Clique, and 7381.92 against 7217.90 on BA-GIANT Max-Cut, each in under five seconds (Table 4, self-reported). Gurobi is not the strongest heuristic for these problems. KaMIS finds 50.94 on ER-1400-1600 in 61 seconds, ahead of COExpander (Table 18). DDEA seeds and recombines an evolutionary algorithm with DIFUSCO samples. It reports MIS sets 7.5% larger than Gurobi's on ER-700-800 under equal time limits ([repository](https://github.com/jsalvasoler/difusco_ddea), self-reported).

### Routing, scheduling and diffusion inside search

On vehicle routing the verified evidence runs against diffusion. COExpander trails HGS by 3.90% to 5.34% on CVRP, worse than a reinforcement-learning constructor given the same local search (Table 6). We found no diffusion result on Solomon or Gehring-Homberger time-window instances. For job shops we found no diffusion result on the Taillard instances, where CP-SAT's upper bounds average 1.02 times the best known in ten minutes. For flow shops we found only a diffusion-enhanced reinforcement-learning framework for dynamic hybrid flow shops ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417426027272), snippet).

Diffusion already serves as the repair step in destroy-and-repair search, but only for TSP and graph problems: T2T's noise-and-reconstruct search, GenSCO's disruption-and-regeneration cycles, and the iterative refinement in DEITSP and StruDiCO are large neighbourhood search in all but name. DIFUSCO-LNS applies the same idea to integer programs, learning which variables to free ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=9QV7Q9gKl9), snippet, self-reported). We found no diffusion-guided neighbourhood search for CVRP, VRPTW or scheduling.

### Where diffusion wins, ties and loses

Diffusion wins on graph problems only against time-limited Gurobi: narrowly on Max-Cut for DiffUCO, and more clearly in COExpander's self-reported large-graph results. KaMIS still leads on MIS. On uniform Euclidean TSP up to 1,000 cities, diffusion trades quality for speed. Measured per instance on one benchmark, diffusion solvers with 2-opt run roughly 7 to 120 times faster than Concorde's exact solve at 500 and 1,000 cities, depending on sampling, but finish 0.25 to 1.8 points worse (COExpander Table 3). It ties only with the handicapped LKH wrapper. Against a correctly configured LKH-3 at 0.002% to 0.005% in under five seconds, every independently measured diffusion gap is worse, and the GenSCO and EDISCO claims remain unreplicated. Diffusion loses on capacitated routing by four to five points to HGS and trails LKH on asymmetric TSP. It has no published time-window or scheduling result to set against PyVRP or CP-SAT. For this report's thesis, a model trained on a firm's historical plans might serve as a warm start or neighbourhood generator for a classical solver, but that is an untested hypothesis. The closest evidence is DDEA's ablation, which found diffusion initialisation helped an evolutionary algorithm on MIS. We found no study in which seeding LKH-3, HGS or CP-SAT with diffusion output beats the classical solver alone at equal wall-clock time, and Xia's all-zeros result points the other way.

### Evidence gaps

arXiv, OpenReview PDFs and the conference proceedings sites were blocked from our environment. We read the Xia, COExpander and DiffUCO papers, full-text copies of the DIFUSCO and Fast T2T papers, and the ML4CO-Bench-101 tables. Everything marked (snippet) comes from search excerpts. We could not verify DISCO's TSP-10000 gap, T2T's own published figures, DEITSP's or GenSCO's runtimes and hardware, the LKH configuration behind Fast T2T's and GenSCO's speed claims, MatDIFFNet's or StruDiCO's absolute gaps, SDDS's objective values, or FrontierCO's per-solver tables. Independent comparison against LKH-3 exists only in Xia and colleagues, Beyond the Heatmap, FrontierCO and COExpander's re-runs of DIFUSCO. We found no diffusion solver evaluated on proprietary industrial routing data, which is the setting the user's idea requires.
