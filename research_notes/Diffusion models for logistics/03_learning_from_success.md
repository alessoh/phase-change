## Learning from past successes works, but the value lies in coarse structure, and no diffusion model has yet been trained on real executed logistics plans

The premise is that a generative model trained on a firm's record of good logistics decisions would absorb what "good" looks like. It has real support: a public benchmark was built on it, many research lines learn from executed routes, and diffusion models learn well from human demonstrations in robotics. Yet no published diffusion model trained on real executed delivery routes or dispatch plans was found in any venue, repository or dataset registry searched up to September 2026, and in this report's prototype a plain supervised network imitates drivers better than its diffusion twin.

### What the Amazon challenge rewarded

The 2021 Amazon Last Mile Routing Research Challenge, run by Amazon with MIT's Center for Transportation and Logistics, released 9,184 routes driven in 2018 in five US metropolitan areas, 6,112 for training and 3,072 for evaluation, with obfuscated locations ([AWS registry entry](https://github.com/awslabs/open-data-registry/blob/main/datasets/amazon-last-mile-challenges.yaml); formal description by Merchán et al. in [*Transportation Science* 58(1)](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2022.1173)). This report's count of the [public bucket](https://amazon-last-mile-challenges.s3.amazonaws.com/?list-type=2) finds 17 training stations and 3,052 evaluation routes. Winners were announced in July 2021 and shared $175,000: $100,000 to William Cook, Stephan Held and Keld Helsgaun, $50,000 to "Permission Denied" (Xiaotong Guo, Qingyi Wang and Baichuan Mo) and $25,000 to "Sky is the Limit" (Okan Arslan and Rasit Abay) ([MIT CTL challenge site](https://routingchallenge.mit.edu/last-mile-routing-research-challenge-2021-winners), snippet).[^finalists]

Success meant matching the driver, not saving time. For each route the official score is SD × ERP<sub>norm</sub> / ERP<sub>e</sub>: the sequence deviation of the proposal from the driver's sequence, multiplied by an edit distance with real penalty computed on normalised travel times, divided by the number of edits that distance prescribes. A submission's score is "the simple, unweighted average of all route scores", a perfect match scores 0, and "complete random shuffles ... typically receive scores between 0.8 and 1.2" ([rc-cli scoring README](https://github.com/MIT-CAVE/rc-cli/blob/main/scoring/README.md)). Travel times enter only inside the edit penalty; there is no term for the total duration of the proposed route and none for computing time. Computation was a hard limit, 12 hours to build a model and 4 hours to apply it ([rc-cli templates README](https://github.com/MIT-CAVE/rc-cli/blob/main/templates/README.md)). Against the random-shuffle anchor, a score of 0.09 is already close to the driver, so differences among leading entries are differences in fine sequence detail.

The data also carry a route quality rating, which "is based both on the level of time window adherence and the amount of backtracking" ([rc-cli data structures](https://github.com/MIT-CAVE/rc-cli/blob/main/templates/data_structures.md)). Of the training routes, 2,718 are High, 3,292 Medium and 102 Low. The label is confounded: in this report's count, High routes average 139.6 delivery stops against 153.0 for Medium, and the High share ranges by station from 28.3 percent (DBO3 and DCH4) to 78.4 percent (DSE2), so the rating partly reflects workload and place.

The primary source for the finalists' methods is the [Technical Proceedings](https://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/131235) edited by Winkenbach, Parks and Noszek, with 31 submitted articles. This report could not open them; the second- and third-place descriptions come from Zattoni Scroccaro et al.'s summary, which says both teams "extracted rules" from drivers' behaviour and tuned multipliers so that TSP routes "enforce the rules previously extracted" ([full text](https://github.com/MohajerinEsfahani/MohajerinEsfahani.github.io/blob/main/Publications/journal/2023/IO_Amazon.pdf)). No podium entry trained a neural network. A later preprint reports that predicting the zone sequence perfectly, even with stops shuffled inside each zone, already yields about 0.0206 ([arXiv 2302.02102](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.02102), snippet). Most attainable fidelity therefore lies in which neighbourhood follows which.

### Drivers' tacit knowledge and deviations

Ziebart and colleagues' PROCAB model learned "the route preferences of 25 taxi drivers demonstrated in over 100,000 miles of collected data" ([Navigate like a cabbie, UbiComp 2008](https://github.com/boudinfl/acm-cr/blob/main/data/acm-dl/ubicomp/ubicomp-2008.bib)), and Ceikute and Jensen compared [local driver behaviour against routing services](https://doi.org/10.1109/MDM.2013.20) (title only). In delivery, Mo, Wang, Guo, Winkenbach and Zhao write that drivers "frequently deviate from planned delivery routes because of their tacit knowledge of the road and curbside infrastructure, customer availability" and train a pair-wise attention pointer network on the Amazon data, reporting about 15 percent lower disparity than benchmark models ([abstract mirror](https://github.com/alexfanjn/arXiv-daily-graphMining/blob/main/info/23-Jan/11/paper_info.json), self-reported). Cainiao's Graph2Route observes "significant deviations" between couriers' actual and planned routes ([KDD 2022 abstract mirror](https://github.com/swkim101/cspapers.org/blob/92397bd6e2418b494f37b1c102c6706050b70480/index2/2022/kdd/Graph2Route%3A%20A%20Dynamic%20Spatial-Temporal%20Graph%20Neural%20Network%20for%20Pick-up%20and%20Delivery%20Route%20Prediction)), Deutsche Post derives precedence constraints from history to capture "the tacit knowledge of experienced couriers" ([*INFORMS Journal on Applied Analytics*](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/inte.2023.0031), snippet), and Dieter, Caron and Schryen integrate driver behaviour into routing "in a hybrid decision support framework" combining machine learning and optimisation ([*EJOR* 311(1)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2023.04.043), title only).

Whether deviations help is contested: Martínez-de-Albéniz and Krigul report from 1.4 million Shanghai deliveries that deviations lengthen routes, especially for inexperienced couriers ([working paper](https://blog.iese.edu/martinezdealbeniz/files/2025/02/Human_Machine_Last_Mile_Deliveries_web.pdf), snippet). This report compared 1,000 Amazon driver sequences with LKH tours minimising travel time: drivers took 14.9 percent longer and re-entered zones 0.8 times per route against 13.1 for LKH, and were late at 1.7 percent of time-windowed stops against 5.9 percent for LKH, which was never given the windows. No study found measures whether plans from a driver-imitating model lower real operational delivery cost once deployed.

### Inverse optimisation, preference learning and imitation

Three families learn from executed plans. Inverse optimisation assumes the decision-maker optimises an unknown objective and recovers it ([Ahuja and Orlin 2001](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.49.5.771.10607); survey by [Chan, Mahmood and Zhu, *Operations Research* 73(2)](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.2022.0382)). Zattoni Scroccaro and colleagues introduced the "incenter" cost and an "Augmented Suboptimality Loss" for inconsistent data ([abstract mirror](https://github.com/qhduan/cn-chat-arxiv/blob/master/papers/23/05/2305.07730.json)), then tailored that loss to routing with an affine hypothesis that adds penalties for leaving a zone area or region. A general version scored 0.0535, the tailored one 0.0302, and training on only 20 percent of the data still gave 0.0334 ([full text](https://github.com/MohajerinEsfahani/MohajerinEsfahani.github.io/blob/main/Publications/journal/2023/IO_Amazon.pdf), self-reported).
Preference learning on routing began before the challenge with Canoy and Guns, ["Vehicle Routing by Learning from Historical Solutions"](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-30048-7_4) at CP 2019, which uses a Markov chain to learn how likely each arc is to appear in a planner's solution ([as summarised by Zattoni Scroccaro et al.](https://github.com/MohajerinEsfahani/MohajerinEsfahani.github.io/blob/main/Publications/journal/2023/IO_Amazon.pdf)). Its extension, Learn-n-route, followed as a January 2021 preprint (later [*Constraints* 28(3)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10601-023-09363-2)), and the neural follow-up by Mandi, Canoy, Bucarey and Guns appeared at [CP 2021](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CP.2021.42), after the challenge.

Inverse reinforcement learning treats behaviour as near-optimal in a sequential decision problem; Ziebart's maximum-entropy version gives a "globally normalized distribution over decision sequences" suited to "inherently noisy" driving data ([AAAI 2008 abstract mirror](https://github.com/swkim101/cspapers.org/blob/92397bd6e2418b494f37b1c102c6706050b70480/index/0/6/336219)). Google Maps scaled IRL to problems it describes as having "hundreds of millions of states and demonstration trajectories" and reports "a 16-24% improvement in route quality at a global scale" ([ICLR 2024 abstract mirror](https://github.com/swkim101/cspapers.org/blob/92397bd6e2418b494f37b1c102c6706050b70480/index2/2024/iclr/Massively%20Scalable%20Inverse%20Reinforcement%20Learning%20in%20Google%20Maps), self-reported). GAIL skips the recovered cost and extracts "a policy directly from data" by an analogy with generative adversarial networks ([NeurIPS 2016 abstract mirror](https://github.com/swkim101/cspapers.org/blob/92397bd6e2418b494f37b1c102c6706050b70480/index/1/3/16153365)). Plain behavioural cloning of the next step compounds its own errors ([Ross, Gordon and Bagnell 2011](https://github.com/mlresearch/v15/blob/gh-pages/_posts/2011-06-14-ross11a.md)), an argument for generating whole plans.

The label source is the crucial distinction. Neural combinatorial optimisation has imitated solvers from the start: Pointer Networks learned the TSP from Held-Karp and heuristic tours, and a network trained on the weakest heuristic "outperforms the algorithm that is trying to imitate" ([NeurIPS 2015 full-text mirror](https://github.com/ilivans/ysda-information-retrieval/blob/5370459e36f0505661431869f5fcfcb531273970/05_language_model/txt/5866-pointer-networks.txt)). Solver labels are consistent and share the objective used for judging; executed plans are noisy, embed unrecorded constraints, and are judged by fidelity or by outcomes the data only partly record.

### Diffusion learning from demonstrations

Diffusion Policy by Chi and colleagues, the strongest precedent, represents a robot policy as a conditional denoising process trained on human demonstrations and reports an average 46.9 percent improvement across 12 tasks ([RSS 2023 abstract mirror](https://github.com/swkim101/cspapers.org/blob/92397bd6e2418b494f37b1c102c6706050b70480/index2/2023/rss/Diffusion%20Policy%3A%20Visuomotor%20Policy%20Learning%20via%20Action%20Diffusion), self-reported; journal version in the [*International Journal of Robotics Research*, 2024](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/docs/source/policy_diffusion_README.md)). The authors attribute the gain to "gracefully handling multimodal action distributions", suitability for "high-dimensional action spaces" and "impressive training stability". Those advantages matter for routing only where history is genuinely multimodal, with several distinct acceptable orders for the same inputs. DiffTraj denoises GPS trajectories into "privacy-free alternatives" to real data ([NeurIPS 2023 abstract mirror](https://github.com/swkim101/cspapers.org/blob/92397bd6e2418b494f37b1c102c6706050b70480/index2/2023/neurips/DiffTraj%3A%20Generating%20GPS%20Trajectory%20with%20Diffusion%20Probabilistic%20Model)), but a trip is continuous motion, whereas a delivery plan is a permutation under time windows and capacity. Diffusion solvers for routing, such as DIFUSCO, train on Concorde and LKH-3 tours, not executed plans ([NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/0ba520d93c3df592c83a611961314c98-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

The closest generative precedent on real executed routes is Liu et al., ["Can language models be used for real-world urban-delivery route optimization?"](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xinn.2023.100520) (*The Innovation*, 2023), listed in the dataset registry among works using the Amazon routes; its text could not be opened, so its architecture and scores are unverified. With it in view the conclusion stands: generative sequence models have been applied to real executed delivery routes, but no published diffusion model has.

### This report's prototype

An unpublished project prototype trains a DIFUSCO-style edge model on 2,508 High routes at two levels, stop order and zone order, with OR-Tools sequencing stops inside the generated zone order; the identical network is also trained as a one-shot supervised classifier. The supervised network beats diffusion at both levels on every seed, and zone-level learners beat the non-learned baselines with shorter tours than drivers', so the gain comes from learning the right structure, not from diffusion. The multimodality argument explains why. Across all training routes, when two zones at the same station appear in multiple routes, drivers visit them in the majority order 92.7 percent of the time, ranging from 82.2 percent at DCH2 to 95.8 percent at DBO3 (this report's count). Zone order is close to deterministic per station, so a unimodal edge classifier loses little, while diffusion's sampling noise costs fidelity.

### Label quality: what counts as a success

A firm's history mixes good, ordinary and poor plans. Rather than discarding weak plans, a model can condition on measured outcomes, as Decision Diffuser does by modelling a policy "as a return-conditional diffusion model" ([ICLR 2023 abstract mirror](https://github.com/swkim101/cspapers.org/blob/92397bd6e2418b494f37b1c102c6706050b70480/index2/2023/iclr/Is%20Conditional%20Generative%20Modeling%20all%20you%20need%20for%20Decision%20Making%3F)). Robotics warns that offline methods "struggle to deal with mixed quality human data" ([robomimic, CoRL 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v164/mandlekar22a.html), snippet). Neither has been tested on routing, and the Amazon rating shows that a success label can encode workload and geography. Success should be defined from measured, workload-adjusted outcomes such as on-time rate and cost per stop.

### Conditioning, data volume, rights and privacy

No paper found conditions a routing generator on industry or company; the working unit is the depot, as when Zattoni Scroccaro et al. learn "separately for each depot" because each zone is always served by one depot. Heterogeneity is large: Amazon stations contributed 60 to 1,133 training routes, High shares vary nearly threefold, and zone-order consistency varies by station. LaDe spans five Chinese cities from Shanghai to small Yantai, with 10,677,000 packages from 21,000 couriers ([repository](https://github.com/wenhaomin/LaDe)), the natural testbed for cross-city conditioning, but its README reports no per-city or transfer results. The best volume evidence is that inverse optimisation kept its ranking with 20 percent of the Amazon data; no study measures how diffusion imitation scales with routes per depot.

The Amazon data carry a non-commercial CC BY-NC 4.0 licence ([registry entry](https://github.com/awslabs/open-data-registry/blob/main/datasets/amazon-last-mile-challenges.yaml)), so a business would train on its own routes, which are personal data: the GDPR lists "location data" as an identifier ([Article 4(1)](http://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex:32016R0679), snippet). DiffTraj's "privacy-free" framing needs qualifying, because Carlini and colleagues extracted memorised training images from diffusion models and warned that "synthetic data does not give privacy for free" ([USENIX Security 2023, full-text mirror](https://github.com/anlp-team/LTI_Neural_Navigator/blob/main/final_data/Daphne%20Ippolito_Extracting%20Training%20Data%20from%20Diffusion%20Models.txt)). Mitigations include differentially private diffusion training, so far shown on image benchmarks ([Dockhorn et al., TMLR 2023, code](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/DPDM)), location obfuscation of the kind Amazon applied, and aggregation to zone level, where most learnable value appears to lie.

### Evidence gaps

The podium, prizes and the winners' 0.0248 score rest on organiser pages seen only as search snippets. The finalists' papers in the Technical Proceedings were not read, so the second- and third-place methods are known through Zattoni Scroccaro et al.'s summary, and Mo et al.'s absolute challenge score is unknown. The texts of Liu et al., Dieter et al., Ceikute and Jensen, and Canoy and Guns could not be opened. Practitioner evidence on drivers overriding UPS's ORION routes ([Holland et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1287/inte.2016.0875)) could not be verified. No published measurement shows whether driver-imitating plans lower real delivery cost. The prototype is unpublished, and the absence of diffusion trained on real logistics plans covers public work only.

[^finalists]: Organisers report 229 teams. One organiser page says 45 submissions reached the finalist phase while another passage, and Zattoni Scroccaro et al., refer to 48 qualifying models; this report cannot reconcile the figures.
