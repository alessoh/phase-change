## A diffusion model trained on past plans only proposes routes; search and classical repair make them feasible, and how much the learned proposal adds is still contested

A common intuition is that a model which has absorbed a business's past successes will "know what to expect" and can produce good plans unaided. The evidence up to September 2026 supports a narrower claim. A diffusion solver emits scores for links between stops, or a noisy candidate solution, not a dispatchable plan. Every competitive system adds decoding, search or repair, and controlled studies disagree about how much of the final quality the learned part contributes. Time windows, capacities and fleets are where pure diffusion is weakest. Results reported by a method's own authors are marked (self-reported), and claims seen only in search excerpts are marked (snippet).

### From heatmap to tour

DIFUSCO, the reference diffusion solver for routing, denoises a binary vector over edges into an edge "heatmap" ([Sun and Yang, NeurIPS 2023, paper text mirror](https://github.com/Xiao-ao-jiang-hu/auto-paper/blob/main/output/DIFUSCO_Graph-based_Diffusion_Solvers_for_Combinatorial_Opti/paper_content.md)). Its code builds a tour by greedy edge insertion, ranking edges by score divided by distance and skipping any edge that would close a loop early, then applies GPU-batched 2-opt ([tsp_utils.py](https://github.com/Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO/blob/main/difusco/utils/tsp_utils.py)). With Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS), which uses the heatmap to steer k-opt moves, the paper reports gaps of 0.46%, 1.17% and 2.58% on 500, 1,000 and 10,000 stops (self-reported). Xia and colleagues, unable to obtain DIFUSCO's heatmaps, retrained it and measured 0.51%, 1.18% and 2.77%, flagging their reproduction for "potential overestimation of reported gaps due to issues found in their code" ([Xia et al., ICML 2024, Table 1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v235/main/assets/xia24f/xia24f.pdf)). Beam search, which keeps the most probable partial tours at each step, was the decoder of Joshi and colleagues' earlier graph network ([code](https://github.com/chaitjo/graph-convnet-tsp)); applied to 500 stops it sits 83.55% above optimal, worse than its greedy 79.61% (Xia, Table 1, quoting earlier work). An independent re-run of a similar network with 2-opt reached 1.35% ([COExpander, ICML 2025, Table 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/ma25r/ma25r.pdf)), so local search matters more than the decoder.

### How much does the learned heatmap add?

Xia and colleagues replaced learned heatmaps with SoftDist, a softmax over distances with one tuned temperature, and ran all heatmaps through identical MCTS on one 64-core CPU. SoftDist reached 1.44%, 2.20% and 3.13% (self-reported). Under the tuned MCTS settings of Min and colleagues, 500-stop gaps nearly converge: DIFUSCO 0.90%, SoftDist 1.03%, and an all-zero heatmap 1.06% (Xia, Tables 1 and 3). Their Score metric compares MCTS with LKH-3, whose runs were adjusted to "align the search time with MCTS", that is, MCTS search time only, excluding heatmap generation; DIFUSCO's Score was 2.39%, 7.78% and 33.82% (Table 2).

This finding is disputed. Yimeng Min's published comment argues that Xia and colleagues did not run all baseline steps on the same hardware and measured time inconsistently, and that "the primary claim made in SoftDist is no longer supported" once they do ([arXiv 2406.09441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09441), abstract only). A separate study, first submitted as "Rethinking the 'Heatmap + Monte Carlo Tree Search' Paradigm" ([OpenReview TMHOHRR0FA](https://openreview.net/forum?id=TMHOHRR0FA), rejected; abstract via [review dataset mirror](https://github.com/weathon/split_review_2/blob/main/datasets/deepreview_13k_train/human_reviews/TMHOHRR0FA.md)) and published at ICLR 2026 as "Beyond the Heatmap" ([OpenReview H6PLJnnK6e](https://openreview.net/forum?id=H6PLJnnK6e)), finds that MCTS configuration "profoundly influences" quality and that a parameter-free k-nearest heatmap with tuned MCTS reaches about 0.50%, 0.85% and 2.1% ([digest](https://github.com/memgrafter/research-digests/blob/main/ml_research_analysis_2024/2411.09238_beyond-the-heatmap-a-rigorous-evaluation-of-component-impact-in-mcts-based-tsp-solvers_20260215_064926.md), snippet, self-reported). The fair reading is that the learned heatmap's value under strong search is contested, and that heatmap comparisons are meaningless unless search is tuned equally for each.

### Guidance, projection and reward

Later work moves search inside generation. T2T adds noise to part of a solution and denoises it under gradient guidance from the tour-cost objective, a test-time energy guidance ([T2T paper text mirror](https://github.com/Xiao-ao-jiang-hu/auto-paper/blob/main/output/%E2%AD%90T2T_From_Distribution_Learning_in_Training_to_Gradient_Sear/paper_content.md)). Fast T2T distills this into a consistency model and claims that one-step generation plus one-step gradient search outperforms diffusion solvers needing hundreds of steps "while achieving tens of times speedup", "even outperforming LKH given limited time budgets" ([Fast T2T paper text mirror](https://github.com/Xiao-ao-jiang-hu/auto-paper/blob/main/output/%E2%AD%90Fast_T2T_Optimization_Consistency_Speeds_Up_Diffusion-Based/paper_content.md), self-reported). That LKH comparison used a Python wrapper whose SPECIAL setting disables LKH's 2-opt and 3-opt moves; corrected, LKH reaches 0.002% on 500 stops in 1.85 seconds (COExpander, Appendix D.2 and Table 11). "Leveraging Structural Constraints for Diffusion-based Neural TSP Solvers" replaces gradient refinement with retraining-free projection to valid tours plus 2-opt, reporting 0.17% and 0.31% on 500 and 1,000 stops with up to 30 to 40% less inference time ([arXiv 2606.09343, abstract mirror](https://github.com/ComDec/Paper_Daily/blob/main/daily_html/2026_06_09.html), self-reported). COExpander fixes the most confident part of a solution and re-predicts the rest, and NExCO (ICLR 2026) makes this the unmasking process of a masked diffusion model "under feasibility projection" ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=084SvT55yk), snippet, self-reported).

Reward and energy guidance also enter training. DiffUCO trains a diffusion sampler from the objective alone, with no solved examples ([Sanokowski et al., ICML 2024](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v235/main/assets/sanokowski24a/sanokowski24a.pdf)), and SDDS makes this memory-efficient through policy-gradient and importance-sampling objectives, reporting state-of-the-art results on six of seven unsupervised benchmarks, all graph problems rather than routing ([ICLR 2025 paper text mirror](https://github.com/Xiao-ao-jiang-hu/auto-paper/blob/main/output/Scalable_Discrete_Diffusion_Samplers_Combinatorial_Optimizat/paper_content.md), self-reported). CADO fine-tunes DIFUSCO with reinforcement learning on tour cost, reporting 0.88% against 1.86% on 1,000 stops ([arXiv 2602.08210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08210), snippet, self-reported), and Combinatorial Adjoint Matching (ICML 2026) trains without labels and ships CVRP utilities ([code](https://github.com/Shengyu-Feng/CAM)). General constrained samplers, such as Projected Diffusion Models ([NeurIPS 2024](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/95942), snippet) and Constrained Discrete Diffusion ([NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/119091), snippet), have not been shown on routing, and a July 2026 study found learned proposals lost their advantage once variables coupled ([arXiv 2607.27169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27169), snippet).

### Time windows, capacities and fleets

Whether a link is acceptable depends on the load on board and the arrival time, both functions of the route so far, which a static edge heatmap cannot express. COExpander's authors write that no global prediction solver can yet handle "relatively complex constraints, such as CVRP (with demand constraints)". On 50 to 500 customers their one-shot predictor was 21.8% to 28.5% above HGS, adaptive expansion 12.3% to 18.2%, and expansion plus classical local search 3.9% to 5.3%, behind a reinforcement-learning constructor with the same local search at 1.9% to 4.2% (Appendix H, Table 26, self-reported). Masking is the dependable route to capacity feasibility: DeepACO multiplies its heatmap by visit and capacity masks at each step ([code](https://github.com/henry-yeh/DeepACO/blob/main/cvrp/aco.py)). Time windows are harder because a locally feasible step can leave no feasible completion; the non-diffusion PIP framework adds preventative masks for this ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Ktx95ZuRjP), snippet, self-reported).

Fleets raise a representation problem. In an edge heatmap the depot has many incident edges, one pair per route, and nothing labels which vehicle drives which route. Vehicles are interchangeable in the heatmap, so it cannot express a heterogeneous fleet, fleet-size limits or depot assignment without extra structure; this is our analysis, not a published result. The closest diffusion work generates a "constraint assignment matrix" that guides an autoregressive CVRP solver ([arXiv 2603.07568](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.07568v1), snippet, self-reported). By contrast PyVRP natively supports heterogeneous fleets, multiple depots and time windows, reporting 0.51% and 0.81% above best-known on heterogeneous-fleet and multi-depot time-window benchmarks ([README](https://github.com/PyVRP/PyVRP); [benchmarks](https://github.com/PyVRP/PyVRP/blob/main/docs/source/setup/benchmarks.rst), self-reported). We found no diffusion result on Solomon or Gehring and Homberger time-window instances.

| Mechanism | Guarantees | Capacity | Time windows | Multiple or mixed vehicles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Greedy, beam, 2-opt or MCTS on a heatmap | A valid single tour | No | No | No |
| Masked construction (DeepACO style) | Valid by construction | Yes | Only with exact masks | Not shown |
| Adaptive expansion, masked diffusion | Per-step checks (self-reported) | Checked, weak quality | Not shown | Not shown |
| Gradient, energy or reward guidance | Lower cost, no hard guarantee | Not shown | Not shown | Not shown |
| Classical repair or polish (HGS, PyVRP, LKH-3) | Exact feasibility checks | Yes | Yes | Yes |

The practical design is hybrid: the diffusion sample becomes a warm start or candidate-edge list for a classical engine whose checks are exact.

### Compute, latency and scale

DIFUSCO trains with a 1,000-step diffusion horizon but samples with 50 steps at inference; its paper trains 500-stop models on 128,000 LKH-labeled instances for 50 epochs on eight V100 GPUs (paper text; [scripts](https://github.com/Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO/blob/main/reproducing_scripts.md) set `--inference_diffusion_steps 50`, with some evaluation configurations at 25 epochs). Fast T2T reports 142 GPU-hours on one A100 for 500 stops, 324 for 1,000, peak memory of 20 to 38 GB, and about double DIFUSCO's training time and memory (Table 11, self-reported). COExpander labeled up to 1,280,000 CVRP instances with HGS and ran on 80 GB H800 GPUs.

Latency figures are easily misread. DIFUSCO's greedy heatmap times of 3.61, 11.86 and 28.51 minutes, reused by Xia, are totals over the 128, 128 and 16 test instances (DIFUSCO's metric definition; test counts per [T2T](https://github.com/Xiao-ao-jiang-hu/auto-paper/blob/main/output/%E2%AD%90T2T_From_Distribution_Learning_in_Training_to_Gradient_Sear/paper_content.md) and [UDC](https://github.com/Xiao-ao-jiang-hu/auto-paper/blob/main/output/UDC_A_Unified_Neural_Divide-and-Conquer_Framework_for_Large-/paper_content.md)), about 1.7, 5.6 and 107 seconds per instance on a V100. Independently re-run on an H800, DIFUSCO with 2-opt took 2.78 seconds per 500-stop instance, and Fast T2T with five steps 0.33 seconds at 0.91% (COExpander, Table 3). Fast T2T's own totals imply about 0.12 seconds per 500-stop instance for one step plus 2-opt at 1.23%, against about 3.0 seconds for T2T with 50 steps and 30 guided steps at 0.82% (Table 2, self-reported). DISCO denoises in few analytic steps and claims inference up to 5.28 times faster than other diffusion solvers ([OpenReview abstract mirror](https://github.com/weathon/split_review_2/blob/main/datasets/deepreview_13k_calibration/6JDpWJrjyK.md), self-reported; rejected at ICLR 2025).

Scale is limited by the quadratic heatmap. DIFUSCO keeps 50 candidate edges per stop at 500 stops and 100 at 1,000 and 10,000, with batch size one at 10,000 (scripts). DISCO adds divide-and-conquer to reach unseen sizes (same abstract), and non-diffusion partitioners such as GLOP ([README](https://github.com/henry-yeh/GLOP)) and UDC go further. At 10,000 stops the best learned entry in ML4CO-Bench-101 is 1.45% behind LKH with only 500 trials, in 33.5 against 332.8 seconds ([Table 8](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/ML4CO-Bench-101/blob/main/assets/performance.png), self-reported). Every diffusion result at 10,000 stops is on uniform random Euclidean TSP; none covers capacitated or time-windowed fleets.

### Generalization across regions and industries

Shift hurts even synthetically: the best learned 500-stop result rises from 0.257% on uniform data to 1.998% on clustered data ([ML4CO-Bench-101 Table 9](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/ML4CO-Bench-101/blob/main/assets/performance_cross.png), self-reported). Amazon's 2021 challenge data, 9,184 driver-executed routes from five US metros, is described as "the first large and publicly available dataset" of real operational routing ([registry](https://github.com/awslabs/open-data-registry/blob/main/datasets/amazon-last-mile-challenges.yaml)); Cainiao's LaDe adds 10,677,000 packages from 21,000 couriers ([KDD 2024 abstract mirror](https://github.com/swkim101/cspapers.org/blob/92397bd6e2418b494f37b1c102c6706050b70480/index2/2024/kdd/LaDe%3A%20The%20First%20Comprehensive%20Last-mile%20Express%20Dataset%20from%20Industry)). We found no study training a diffusion router in one region or industry and testing in another.

### A fair protocol for judging a diffusion solver

Contenders should include HGS through PyVRP, correctly configured LKH-3 and OR-Tools; PyVRP puts OR-Tools at 4.42% and 9.38% above best-known on CVRP and VRPTW against 0.31% and 0.65% for PyVRP 0.14.0 (benchmarks, self-reported). PyVRP's template scales time limits by CPU PassMark score and uses ten seeded single-core runs per instance ([benchmarking](https://github.com/PyVRP/PyVRP/blob/main/docs/source/dev/benchmarking.rst)).

Test data should be real held-out plans split by date, deduplicated, with a separate regional split and the firm's asymmetric road travel times. Every heatmap, learned, SoftDist, k-nearest, all-zero or random, should pass through identical decoding and post-processing, and tuning must be budget-neutral: MCTS parameters and SoftDist temperature are tuned on the same validation set, with the same number of trials, as the diffusion model's hyperparameters. We recommend stricter time parity than Xia's: classical solvers receive the diffusion pipeline's total wall-clock time, including heatmap generation. Because one GPU and one CPU core differ in cost, results should also be reported at equal cloud cost and, where measurable, equal energy, with anytime curves and per-instance latency separate from batched throughput. Feasibility rate belongs beside the gap as a headline metric, and infeasible plans count as failures.

Statistical power needs attention. Standard sets hold only 128 or 16 instances, and DIFUSCO averaged five seeds (paper text). Firms should use hundreds of held-out days, run several training and sampling seeds, report confidence intervals, and use paired tests on per-instance gaps. Finally, experienced planners should rank anonymized plans blind to source. We found no published blind judging of diffusion routing output.

### Evidence gaps

arXiv, OpenReview, Semantic Scholar and the NeurIPS and ICLR proceedings were blocked, so we read Xia, COExpander, DiffUCO, DIFUSCO, T2T, Fast T2T, SDDS and UDC through mirrors, and saw only abstracts or excerpts for Min's comment, Beyond the Heatmap, DISCO, NExCO, CADO, the projection preprint, PIP, the constraint-matrix and July 2026 preprints, and the constrained samplers. Joshi and colleagues' own TSP-50 and TSP-100 figures could not be confirmed and are omitted. We could not verify Fast T2T's timing basis beyond its consistency with quoted totals, DIFUSCO's training GPU-hours, or ML4CO-Bench-101's hardware. We found no diffusion result on time-window, heterogeneous-fleet or multi-depot benchmarks, no equal-cost comparison against PyVRP on real delivery data, and no cross-region transfer study.
