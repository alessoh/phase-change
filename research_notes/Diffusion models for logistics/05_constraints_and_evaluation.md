## Feasibility, cost and fair testing of diffusion solvers

A diffusion model trained on a company's past plans learns where good solutions tend to lie, but what it emits is a probability map or a noisy candidate, not a plan a dispatcher can send. Every competitive published system adds a classical decoding, search or repair stage, and that stage often explains much of the final quality. Hard operational constraints such as time windows remain the weakest point of the approach, compute costs are only partly published, and no study yet shows transfer across regions or industries. The only credible way for a business to judge a diffusion solver is an equal-budget, blind test against the solvers it already trusts.

### From heatmap to tour

DIFUSCO, the reference diffusion solver for routing, outputs a heatmap scoring every edge between two stops. Its code builds a tour by greedy edge insertion, ranking edges by score and skipping any that would close a premature loop, and then offers 2-opt local search and best-of-several sampling ([DIFUSCO repository](https://github.com/Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO)). The decoder matters enormously. In ML4TSPBench's re-run of DIFUSCO on 500 stops, greedy decoding alone lands 9.639% above optimal, greedy plus 2-opt reaches 1.524%, and eight greedy samples plus 2-opt reach 0.686% ([ML4TSPBench Table 9](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/ML4TSPBench/blob/main/docs/figs/iclr25_ml4tsp_results.png)). In a separate evaluation, DIFUSCO's own paper reports 0.46% using Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), a different decoder that uses the heatmap to steer a k-opt search rather than running after greedy decoding ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08224), snippet, self-reported).

T2T moved search inside the generative process. At test time it re-noises part of the current solution and denoises it under the objective's gradient, reconstructing "a lower-cost solution guided by the optimization objective" ([T2T README](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/T2TCO), self-reported), and Fast T2T compresses this into a consistency model whose README example uses a single inference step ([Fast-T2T README](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/Fast-T2T)). GenSCO (NeurIPS 2025) goes further by treating generation as a search operator: each cycle disrupts the current solution with local search moves and then re-samples it with a rectified-flow model trained to map suboptimal solutions toward optimal ones. It reports reaching a 0.02% gap on 500 stops about ten times faster than LKH-3 ([GenSCO README](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/GenSCO), self-reported).

The strongest caution comes from Xia and colleagues at ICML 2024. Under identical MCTS settings on 500 stops, SoftDist, a softmax over distances with one tuned temperature, reached a 1.44% gap, better than Att-GCN at 1.64% though behind their reproduction of DIFUSCO at 0.51%, which spent 3.61 minutes generating heatmaps for the test set where SoftDist needed almost none. With better-tuned MCTS parameters the heatmaps nearly converged: DIFUSCO 0.90%, SoftDist 1.03%, and an all-zero heatmap 1.06%. Their Score metric, which gives LKH-3 the same CPU time, found heatmap-guided MCTS delivering less than a tenth of LKH-3's efficiency at 500 and 1,000 stops ([Xia et al., PMLR v235](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/xia24f.html)). A learned heatmap therefore adds real but modest value, and the search stage carries most of the load.

### Why constraints break the one-shot heatmap

Delivery plans add capacity, time windows, shifts, depots and a fleet. Whether an edge is acceptable depends on the load on board and the clock time on arrival, both functions of the route so far, which a static edge heatmap cannot express. The ML4CO-Bench-101 and COExpander papers give the clearest vocabulary for this. Global Prediction methods, which include DIFUSCO, predict a whole heatmap in one shot and struggle with long-range decisions and constraint conflicts. Local Construction methods build a route stop by stop and stay feasible but scale poorly. Adaptive Expansion, introduced by COExpander (ICML 2025), fixes part of the solution, recomputes the heatmap given that partial plan, and repeats with adaptive step sizes ([COExpander, OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=KMaBXMWsBM), self-reported). NExCO (ICLR 2026) makes this native to a masked diffusion model, expanding confident candidates while "applying feasibility projection to expand the solution while respecting constraints" ([NExCO, OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=084SvT55yk), self-reported). Its abstract names no problem classes, and whether it reports vehicle routing results could not be verified, although awesome-ml4co lists it under vehicle routing ([awesome-ml4co](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/awesome-ml4co)).

Masking during construction guarantees validity for capacity. DeepACO and GFACS, which are not diffusion models, multiply a learned heatmap by visit and capacity masks at every step, so each sampled route respects vehicle capacity ([DeepACO CVRP code](https://github.com/henry-yeh/DeepACO/blob/main/cvrp/aco.py); [GFACS CVRP code](https://github.com/ai4co/gfacs/blob/main/cvrp_nls/aco.py)), and a diffusion heatmap could be decoded the same way. Time windows are different. Bi and colleagues (NeurIPS 2024) show that neural constructors "struggle with handling complex constraints, especially when obtaining the masking itself is NP-hard", because a step that looks feasible can leave no feasible completion. Their PIP framework adds Lagrangian penalties and preventative masks, and PIP-D learns those masks, reducing infeasibility on time-windowed TSP ([PIP, OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Ktx95ZuRjP), self-reported). No diffusion implementation of this was found.

Projection has matured quickly. Projected Diffusion Models (NeurIPS 2024) recast sampling as constrained optimisation and report use on non-convex constraints, including path planning ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=FsdB3I9Y24), self-reported). Constrained Discrete Diffusion (NeurIPS 2025) imposes constraints during discrete sampling without retraining and reports "zero constraint violations" on text and molecule tasks ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Es4s9dtCjR), self-reported). NSPSG (ICML 2026) projects generated graphs with a satisfiability-modulo-theories solver and reports 99 to 100% validity ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=cbtykHVWX9), self-reported). None has been shown on vehicle routing with time windows.

Guidance and fine-tuning steer quality rather than certify feasibility. DIFU-Ada adapts a trained solver to prize-collecting and orienteering variants without retraining ([AAAI 2026](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/40948), snippet, self-reported). CADO fine-tunes a diffusion solver with reinforcement learning, placing cost and the post-processing decoder inside training; it was rejected at ICLR 2025 ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=pbDqZBn2X2)). GuideCO, withdrawn from ICLR 2025, reports "an exponential scaling law between the optimality gap and the amount of training data" and trains an objective-guided model on imperfect labels ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=D3vD7ZFIor)). That is exactly a company's situation, since historical plans are good but not optimal. Diffusion work on vehicle routing is sparse but not absent. Diffusion-Enhanced GFlowNet reported routing results up to 10,000 nodes but was rejected at ICLR 2026 ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=yrJdTtvASl)), and CAM (ICML 2026) ships an unsupervised discrete-diffusion training script for 100-customer CVRP ([CAM repository](https://github.com/Shengyu-Feng/CAM)), whose results were not verified. For hard constraints the practical answer remains a hybrid in which the diffusion sample becomes a warm start or candidate-edge list for HGS, PyVRP or LKH-3, whose load and time checks are exact.

| Mechanism | Guarantee | Capacity | Time windows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Greedy insertion plus 2-opt, or MCTS | Valid single tour | No | No |
| Masked construction (DeepACO, GFACS) | Valid by construction | Yes | Not in general; exact masking can be NP-hard (PIP) |
| Adaptive expansion (COExpander, NExCO) | Feasibility projection per step (self-reported) | Not verified | Not verified |
| Continuous projection (Projected Diffusion Models) | Constraint satisfaction, non-convex use reported | Not shown for routing | Not shown for routing |
| Discrete projection (Constrained Discrete Diffusion, NSPSG) | Zero or near-zero violations on non-routing tasks | Not shown for routing | Not shown for routing |
| Guidance or fine-tuning (DIFU-Ada, CADO, GuideCO) | None hard | Soft preference | Soft preference |
| Classical repair (HGS, PyVRP, LKH-3) | Exact feasibility checks | Yes | Yes |

### Compute, latency and scale

Supervised labels are expensive before any GPU is involved. DIFUSCO's 128,000 training instances of 500 stops are created with a script whose default solver is LKH-3 with 1,000 trials and 10 runs per instance ([generate_tsp_data.py](https://github.com/Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO/blob/main/data/generate_tsp_data.py)); the published commands do not override it, and the "_concorde" suffix in their filenames is only a naming default ([data README](https://github.com/Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO/blob/main/data/README.md)). That is 1.28 million LKH-3 runs. If each takes one to four seconds, as ML4TSPBench's timings for 500 and 5,000 trials suggest, labelling costs roughly 360 to 1,400 CPU-core hours; this is our own estimate. Training scripts use eight GPUs for 50 epochs with 50 denoising steps ([reproducing scripts](https://github.com/Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO/blob/main/reproducing_scripts.md)), Xia and colleagues list DIFUSCO's hardware as eight V100 GPUs, and they measured 3.61 minutes of DIFUSCO heatmap generation for the 128-instance, 500-stop test set and 28.51 minutes at 10,000 stops ([Xia et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/xia24f.html)). Total GPU-hours for training were not found.

Memory is the scaling constraint. A dense edge heatmap for 10,000 stops has 100 million entries, so DIFUSCO keeps only 50 or 100 nearest neighbours per stop, about one million edges, and trains at batch size one on that size. At 10,000 stops ML4CO-Bench-101's best learned entry, COExpander with greedy decoding and 2-opt, is 1.450% above LKH-3 in 33.5 seconds against 332.8 seconds ([Table 8](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/ML4CO-Bench-101/blob/main/assets/performance.png), self-reported, hardware unstated). StruDiCO (NeurIPS 2025) reports up to 3.5 times faster inference and 70% lower GPU memory than earlier diffusion solvers ([StruDiCO](https://github.com/yuuuuwang/StruDiCO), self-reported), and DEITSP's README sets 16 inference steps in its TSP20 training and test examples ([DEITSP](https://github.com/DEITSP/DEITSP)). For thousands of stops with capacities, partitioning is the most developed route. GLOP, a non-diffusion method, reports "reasonable solutions" for TSP up to 100,000 nodes and ships CVRP scripts up to 7,000 ([GLOP README](https://github.com/henry-yeh/GLOP), self-reported). FrontierCO still reports "a persistent performance gap that widens under structurally challenging and large instance sizes" ([FrontierCO](https://github.com/sunnweiwei/FrontierCO)).

### Generalization across distributions and regions

Training on one firm's history is in-distribution learning by design. On clustered 500-stop instances COExpander's gap rises to 1.998%, against 0.257% on the uniform data it was trained for ([cross-distribution table](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/ML4CO-Bench-101/blob/main/assets/performance_cross.png), self-reported). Real road networks produce asymmetric travel times, and here the best learned result in ML4CO-Bench-101 is 1.568% behind LKH-3 on 500-node asymmetric instances ([Table 8](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/ML4CO-Bench-101/blob/main/assets/performance.png), self-reported); how those matrices were generated was not checked. The Amazon Last Mile Routing Research Challenge offers 9,184 real routes from five US metropolitan areas, split into 6,112 training and 3,072 evaluation routes ([registry entry](https://github.com/awslabs/open-data-registry/blob/main/datasets/amazon-last-mile-challenges.yaml); [Transportation Science article](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/trsc.2022.1173)). No study was found training a diffusion routing model in one region or industry and testing it in another.

### A fair protocol for judging a diffusion solver

Accorsi, Lodi and Vigo attributed the slow uptake of learned routing methods partly to incompatible evaluation practice ([Operations Research Letters, 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167637722000244), snippet). A credible test borrows the classical community's discipline. Contenders should include HGS through [PyVRP](https://github.com/PyVRP/PyVRP), LKH-3 and OR-Tools, scored on one objective against best-known solutions with a fixed distance-rounding rule, since PyVRP's benchmarks prescribe round, dimacs or exact rounding per instance set and the choice changes gaps. PyVRP's CVRP budget of 2.4n seconds on a reference CPU with PassMark score 2,183, scaled to the tester's CPU, with ten seeded single-core runs per instance, is a sensible default ([PyVRP benchmarking](https://github.com/PyVRP/PyVRP/blob/main/docs/source/dev/benchmarking.rst)).

Test data should be real held-out plans split by date, with near-duplicates removed, a regional split to measure transfer, and the company's actual asymmetric road-network matrices rather than straight-line distances. Every heatmap, including SoftDist, a zero heatmap and a nearest-neighbour heatmap, should receive identical decoding and post-processing tuned on validation data only, following Xia and colleagues' practice of giving LKH-3 matched time. Results should report anytime curves or the primal integral against HGS and LKH-3 at matched CPU-equivalent budgets, per-instance latency separately from batched throughput with GPU model and core count stated, since published tables mix the two ([benchmark CSV](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/awesome-ml4co/blob/master/data/benchmark_results.csv)), and amortised training and labelling cost. Variance should be reported across training and sampling seeds with paired tests on per-instance gaps, and infeasible plans count as failures. Finally, experienced dispatchers should rank anonymised plans without knowing their source; no published study applying such blind judging to diffusion routing output was found.

### Evidence gaps

DIFUSCO's own MCTS figure, DIFU-Ada, FrontierCO's instance results and the Accorsi critique rest on search snippets, and most benchmark figures are self-reported. We could not open the NExCO, CAM, CADO, GuideCO or DEG papers, so their routing results, hardware and constraint handling beyond their abstracts are unverified. The hardware behind ML4CO-Bench-101 timings, DIFUSCO's total training GPU-hours, and the label-cost estimate above remain unconfirmed. No time-window comparison of a diffusion solver against PyVRP under equal time, no cross-region transfer study and no blind human evaluation were found.
