## Diffusion now reaches almost every logistics sub-problem, but no company or startup has shown it making a production logistics decision

Diffusion research now touches forecasting, integer programming, routing, scheduling, packing, facility design, robot motion and synthetic data, most deeply in probabilistic forecasting, combinatorial optimization and multi-robot motion, and the first diffusion work that designs warehouse environments appeared at ICML 2026. What is missing everywhere is production: I found no public evidence that any company, large or small, runs a diffusion model to make a live logistics decision. Because arXiv, OpenReview and several other hosts were blocked, abstracts and acceptance status for ICLR, ICML, NeurIPS and AAAI papers were read from the Paper Copilot accepted-paper lists ([paper lists](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists)), with links to the pages they index. Claims seen only in search summaries are marked (snippet), and authors' own figures are marked (self-reported).

### Forecasting and scenario generation

The most natural fit for the learned-from-success hypothesis behind this report is to generate the futures a plan must survive rather than the plan itself. A diffusion model trained on a company's demand history can be sampled hundreds of times to feed a stochastic optimizer, so the model "knows what to expect" while a solver that guarantees feasibility still makes the decision.

Treeffuser is the only method here whose abstract applies diffusion to a logistics decision, "inventory allocation under uncertainty using sales data from Walmart" (self-reported). D3U feeds a point forecaster into a conditional diffusion model and claims "over a 20% improvement" in long-term forecasting. CW-Gen starts diffusion or flow matching from a normal distribution centred on estimated conditional means and covariances. TEDM adapts elucidated diffusion from image synthesis and says its sampling cost is "linear in the forecast horizon". None of these four abstracts names a retail or supply-chain dataset such as M5.

Independent evaluation tempers the older claims. Microsoft's ProbTS benchmark finds that TimeGrad and CSDI "face challenges in extended forecasting horizons" ([benchmark notes](https://github.com/microsoft/ProbTS/blob/main/docs/benchmark/supervised_model/README.md)). I found no independent study showing diffusion forecasters beating simpler baselines on retail series, so maturity here means volume of work, not proven superiority.

At foundation-model scale, no pure diffusion model leads; flow-matching relatives and autoregressive or quantile transformers do. The kinship matters, because flow matching along Gaussian paths belongs to the same family as diffusion and is closely equivalent to it. Sundial, from Tsinghua's THUML (ICML 2025 Oral), uses "a TimeFlow Loss based on flow-matching" to predict the next patch's distribution ([abstract](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/45591)) and reported first place in MASE on GIFT-Eval in May 2025 ([Sundial](https://github.com/thuml/Sundial), self-reported). Aurora (ICLR 2026) forecasts with "Prototype-Guided Flow Matching" ([forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=VVJ6Ck9JBl)). Amazon says Chronos-2, released in October 2025, achieves "the best performance on fev-bench, GIFT-Eval and Chronos Benchmark II amongst pretrained models" and offers SageMaker deployment ([Chronos](https://github.com/amazon-science/chronos-forecasting), self-reported). Google says the decoder-only TimesFM 3.0 ranks first overall on fev-bench and the TIME benchmark and "#1 among all foundation models" on GIFT-Eval ([TimesFM](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm), self-reported). These first-place claims conflict because each is a snapshot of a moving leaderboard taken at a different date. The TimesFM README also restricts 3.0 weights to "non-commercial, non-production use", which bears directly on deployment.

The newer direction ties generation to decisions. Diffusion-DFL, from Georgia Tech and collaborators (ICLR 2026), trains a diffusion model so that downstream decision quality drives learning ([ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/zhao2026iclr-diffusiondfl/), snippet); its code contains no logistics data ([repo](https://github.com/GT-KOALA/Diffusion_DFL)). Supply-chain work is thin: DE-MAPPO (2026) forecasts replenishment lead times with diffusion for multi-agent inventory control ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0098135426000190), snippet). None reports a deployment.

### Weather: operational diffusion came and went

Google DeepMind's GenCast, now WeatherNext 1 Gen, is "Diffusion-based ensemble forecasting for medium-range weather" ([WeatherNext repository](https://github.com/google-deepmind/graphcast)). Its operational successor is not diffusion. The same repository marks WeatherNext 2 "Used Operationally", distributes its feeds through Google Cloud, and names its report "Skillful joint probabilistic weather forecasting from marginals". I could not open that report, but the released code settles the method: WeatherNext 2 is a "Functional Generative Network (FGN)" that injects noise into a single forward pass and trains on a CRPS loss, with no iterative denoising sampler, whereas GenCast's code ships a denoiser and a DPM-Solver++ sampler (`weathernext/weathernext2/fgn.py` and `weathernext/weathernext1_gen/` in that repository). Weather is a logistics input, and I found no account of a logistics firm planning on diffusion weather ensembles.

### Integer programming, routing, scheduling and packing

"Effective Generation of Feasible Solutions for Integer Programming via Guided Diffusion" appeared at KDD 2024 with four authors at Cainiao Network and others at Tsinghua and the University of Manchester ([KDD 2024 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/kdd/kdd2024.json)). An earlier version reports feasibility above 89.7 percent without a solver and a 3.7 to 33.7 percent gap improvement alongside SCIP ([review mirror](https://github.com/weathon/split_review_2/blob/main/datasets/deepreview_13k_train/human_reviews/joMMM9eadc.md), self-reported). No deployment is reported.

The routing line began with DIFUSCO by Zhiqing Sun and Yiming Yang at Carnegie Mellon, a NeurIPS 2023 Spotlight that denoises heatmaps over graph edges ([NeurIPS 2023 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/nips/nips2023.json)). Shanghai Jiao Tong's Thinklab under Junchi Yan now leads it in volume, and its T2T, Fast T2T, GenSCO and StruDiCO evaluate on travelling salesman and independent set problems ([Fast T2T](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/Fast-T2T)). UniCO (ICLR 2025) adds MatDIFFNet for matrix-encoded TSP with asymmetric distances ([forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=yEwakMNIex)), which matters because real road networks are asymmetric, though its experiments cover ATSP, TSP, HCP and SAT rather than CVRP ([UniCO](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/UniCO)). NEXCO (ICLR 2026) turns masked diffusion into solution expansion with feasibility projection for hard constraints and claims about 50 percent better quality and up to four times faster inference on problems its abstract does not name ([forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=084SvT55yk), self-reported). M2GenCO (ICML 2026) pretrains one diffusion solver across TSP, ATSP, independent set, clique and MaxCut ([M2GenCO](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/M2GenCO)). At Carnegie Mellon, Combinatorial Adjoint Matching (ICML 2026) trains discrete diffusion solvers without solved examples ([forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=y6LGSCnNOz)), and its repository includes a CVRP-100 script ([CAM](https://github.com/Shengyu-Feng/CAM)). CAM is thus the only diffusion solver found with public capacitated-routing code, but I could not see its CVRP results or whether it was compared with HGS or LKH-3. JKU Linz contributes unsupervised diffusion samplers, DiffUCO and SDDS ([repo](https://github.com/ml-jku/DIffUCO)). A recent survey notes that heatmap diffusion solvers struggle with capacity constraints ([survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21761v1), snippet).

Scheduling is sequencing, and diffusion over permutations is now active. SymmetricDiffusers (ICLR 2025 Oral) learns permutation distributions and reports results on sorting, jigsaw puzzles and TSP ([forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=EO8xpnW7aX), self-reported), and Soft-Rank Diffusion (ICML 2026) reports its largest gains in "long-sequence and intrinsically sequential settings" ([forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=joXFeeoiRl), self-reported). Neither abstract evaluates job-shop or flow-shop scheduling; the one scheduling item found uses a diffusion prior to guide reinforcement learning in hybrid flow-shop scheduling ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417426027272), snippet).

In packing, Diffusion-CCSP (CoRL 2023, MIT with Stanford's Jiajun Wu) composes one diffusion model per constraint type, and its repository treats packing 3D objects and stacking shapes under stability constraints as separate tasks ([repo](https://github.com/zt-yang/diffusion-ccsp)). FamiPacking calls itself the first diffusion-based 3D bin packing system ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10461157/), snippet, self-reported).

### Warehouse and facility layout

Diffusion Co-Design (DiCoDe), by Hao Xiang Li, Michael Amir and Amanda Prorok, whose lab is in Cambridge, UK ([lab profile](https://github.com/proroklab)), was accepted at ICML 2026 and generates warehouse environment designs with diffusion ([forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=8atKBiXm7u)). It co-optimizes robot policies and layouts: a guided diffusion model proposes configurations, Projected Universal Guidance keeps them within constraints while steering toward reward,. In a warehouse benchmark it reports "39% higher rewards and 66% fewer simulation steps" than the prior state of the art (self-reported). This is the closest facility-scale test of the report's premise, though on a simulated benchmark. Analogues include GSDiff (AAAI 2025, Hefei University of Technology) generates vector floorplans as graphs of walls and rooms ([AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/33904)), and a UC Berkeley diffusion chip placer (ICML 2025) transfers from synthetic circuits to public benchmarks ([repo](https://github.com/vint-1/chipdiffusion), self-reported).

### Robot motion, path finding and delivery vehicles

Warehouse robotics builds on Diffusion Policy ([repo](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy)) and TU Darmstadt's Motion Planning Diffusion ([code](https://github.com/joaoamcarvalho/mpd-public)). MMD, from Carnegie Mellon, composes single-robot diffusion models with classical multi-agent search, was an ICLR 2025 Spotlight ([ICLR 2025 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/iclr/iclr2025.json)), and plans for "dozens of robots" in scenarios "motivated by logistics environments" ([forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=AUCYptvAf3)). Projected diffusion for simultaneous multi-robot planning followed at ICML 2025 ([ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/45205)), and Projected Coupled Diffusion (ICLR 2026) couples pretrained models and projects each step onto hard constraints, reporting "guaranteed constraint satisfaction" in multi-robot planning ([forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=1FEm5JLpvg), self-reported). DiffLNS, a May 2026 preprint, uses discrete diffusion to warm-start the LNS2 path-finding solver and reports 95.8 percent average success across 20 congested settings ([abstract mirror](https://github.com/ycpku/arxiv-daily/blob/main/reports/2026-05-14.md), self-reported).

On the road, the strongest industry-linked work is Diffusion Planner (ICLR 2025 Oral) from Tsinghua University with co-authors at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, the Chinese University of Hong Kong and HAOMO.AI ([project page source](https://github.com/ZhengYinan-AIR/zhengyinan-air.github.io/blob/HEAD/Diffusion-Planner/index.html)). Besides nuPlan, it is evaluated on "our newly collected 200-hour delivery-vehicle driving dataset" ([forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=wM2sfVgMDH)), recorded by "an autonomous logistics delivery vehicle" and supplied by HAOMO.AI, and it claims state-of-the-art closed-loop performance (self-reported). Its successor, Hyper Diffusion Planner from Tsinghua AIR and Xiaomi EV, "is deployed on a real-vehicle platform" and was tested over 200 km of urban roads ([project page source](https://github.com/ZhengYinan-AIR/zhengyinan-air.github.io/blob/HEAD/Hyper-Diffusion-Planner/index.html)). That is road testing of a passenger-car planner, not a documented production logistics deployment. Waymo's MotionDiffuser and SceneDiffuser apply diffusion to multi-agent prediction and simulation rather than delivery planning ([author page](https://github.com/maxjiang93/maxjiang93.github.io); [NeurIPS 2024 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/nips/nips2024.json)).

### Synthetic data, with a privacy caveat

DiffTraj (NeurIPS 2023) synthesizes GPS trajectories that preserve geographic distributions ([code](https://github.com/Yasoz/DiffTraj)). DiffScene (AAAI 2025), from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, Carnegie Mellon and Gatik AI, steers diffusion toward rare safety-critical driving scenarios ([AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/32951), self-reported). Synthetic data is not automatically private: Carlini and colleagues extracted over a thousand training examples from image diffusion models (USENIX Security 2023; [metadata copy](https://github.com/chaosarium/CMU-LTI-RAG/blob/main/collection/papers/Daphne%20Ippolito%20%7C%20Publication%20%7C%20Extracting%20Training%20Data%20from%20Diffusion%20Models.txt)), and none of the logistics-oriented generators above claims differential privacy.

The negative rows rest on a reproducible screen. I loaded the Paper Copilot lists for KDD 2023 and 2024, NeurIPS 2024 and 2025, ICML 2025 and 2026, ICLR 2025 and 2026, and AAAI 2025, the larger files via media.githubusercontent.com, and kept accepted papers whose title or abstract pairs diffusion, denoising or score-based terms with logistics, routing, scheduling, packing, path finding, combinatorial optimization, multi-robot or autonomous-driving terms. No hit carried a KAIST or JD affiliation, and the one Huawei hit concerned driving-scene rendering. The KDD lists lack abstracts and the ICLR and ICML 2026 lists lack affiliations, weakening those screens.

Amazon's DeepFleet, trained on its robot fleet's movement data, is reported to improve robot travel efficiency by 10 percent without diffusion ([Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/blog/amazon-builds-first-foundation-model-for-multirobot-coordination), snippet, self-reported). The best production evidence for a generative model trained on an operator's own history therefore comes from outside diffusion. Diffusion's most deployable role today is as a scenario and synthetic-data engine for conventional optimizers and a proposal generator inside hybrid solvers. The plan that appears on its own remains a research demonstration, and no startup production deployment was found.

### Evidence gaps

With arXiv, OpenReview, IEEE, Hugging Face and several project sites blocked, abstracts come from Paper Copilot lists and README files. I could not open the WeatherNext 2 report, CAM's CVRP results, NEXCO's problem list, the datasets behind D3U, CW-Gen and TEDM, DiffLNS's affiliations, the full DiCoDe benchmark, a primary description of Gatik AI's business, or DeepFleet's primary paper. The screen misses journals and internal work, and absence of public evidence does not prove absence of internal use.
