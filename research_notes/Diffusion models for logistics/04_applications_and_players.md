## Diffusion now reaches almost every logistics sub-problem, but public evidence of it making a live logistics decision is close to nil

Diffusion research now spans forecasting, scenario generation, integer programming, routing, scheduling, packing, layout, robot motion and synthetic data. It is deepest in probabilistic forecasting, graph optimization and multi-robot motion. Production evidence is thin: the one commercial claim found, a packing system linked to Taiwan's FamilyMart, rests on a search summary, and the nearest operational diffusion system touching logistics inputs is a Google weather model whose operational role has since passed to a successor. Claims seen only in search summaries are marked (snippet), authors' own figures (self-reported), and where a Paper Copilot accepted-paper list was the source actually read, the link points to that list.

### Forecasting and scenario generation

The most natural fit for this report's premise is to generate the futures a plan must survive rather than the plan itself: a diffusion model trained on a company's history is sampled many times, and a solver that guarantees feasibility makes the decision.

The first two rows come from TimeGrad's ICML 2021 Table 2, where Zalando's authors report state of the art "on all but the smallest" of six datasets ([PMLR PDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v139/gh-pages/rasul21a/rasul21a.pdf), self-reported). The rest come from Microsoft's ProbTS short-term benchmark, five seeds each ([ProbTS table](https://github.com/microsoft/ProbTS/blob/main/docs/benchmark/supervised_model/figs/short_bench.jpg)). The two sources use different metrics and settings, so rows compare only within a source. Under ProbTS, CSDI wins on Electricity and Wikipedia and TimeGrad on Solar, but a normalizing flow wins on Traffic, and ProbTS concludes that TimeGrad and CSDI "face challenges in extended forecasting horizons" ([ProbTS notes](https://github.com/microsoft/ProbTS/blob/main/docs/benchmark/supervised_model/README.md)). CRPS figures from the primary CSDI (Stanford, [repo](https://github.com/ermongroup/CSDI)) and TSDiff (Amazon, NeurIPS 2023, [repo](https://github.com/amazon-science/unconditional-time-series-diffusion)) papers could not be read, and ProbTS does not include DeepAR or quantile gradient-boosting baselines.

Newer diffusion forecasters keep appearing: TSFlow from Günnemann's group and D3U at ICLR 2025 ([ICLR 2025 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/iclr/iclr2025.json)), TEDM and MMPD at ICLR 2026 ([ICLR 2026 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/iclr/iclr2026.json)), and a Spotlight on non-stationary diffusion at ICML 2025 ([ICML 2025 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/icml/icml2025.json)). Treeffuser from Columbia applies diffusion to Walmart sales for inventory allocation ([abstract](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/96678), snippet), and CDLF forecasts a new product's life cycle from similar past products, claiming gains on Intel SKU data ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20370), snippet, self-reported). At foundation-model scale no pure diffusion model leads: Tsinghua's Sundial uses a flow-matching loss and reported first place on GIFT-Eval in May 2025 ([Sundial](https://github.com/thuml/Sundial), self-reported), while the models vendors ship, Amazon's Chronos-2 and Google's TimesFM, are not diffusion ([Chronos](https://github.com/amazon-science/chronos-forecasting); [TimesFM](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm), both self-reported).

Scenario generation for stochastic programs is thinner than the premise suggests. Diffusion-DFL (ICLR 2026, code from Georgia Tech's KOALA group) trains a diffusion model of uncertain parameters, solves a stochastic optimization on its samples and claims to outperform "strong baselines in decision quality" ([ICLR 2026 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/iclr/iclr2026.json), self-reported), though its code has no logistics data ([repo](https://github.com/GT-KOALA/Diffusion_DFL)). DE-MAPPO uses diffusion to forecast replenishment lead times inside multi-agent inventory control ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0098135426000190), snippet). A preprint on diffusion-based scenario trees for multistage stochastic optimization ([arXiv 2509.14832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14832)) and the diffusion scenario literature in renewable energy could not be opened. No study was found that shows diffusion scenarios producing cheaper decisions than sample average approximation on historical data, bootstrapping or moment matching.

Weather is the nearest production case. Google DeepMind's repository lists GenCast as "WeatherNext Gen: Diffusion-based ensemble forecasting" and marks WeatherNext 2, documented through an FGN technical report, as "Used Operationally" ([README](https://github.com/google-deepmind/graphcast)). The GenCast documentation ships a checkpoint that "can make predictions in an operational setting" ([WeatherNext Gen docs](https://github.com/google-deepmind/graphcast/blob/main/docs/weathernext1_gen/README.md)). The README gives no reason for the change and does not describe WeatherNext 2's mechanism, and whether WeatherNext Gen forecasts were sold as a Google Cloud product before WeatherNext 2 could not be confirmed.

### Integer programming, routing and scheduling

The most industry-linked optimization paper is guided diffusion for feasible integer-programming solutions (KDD 2024), listed with seven authors ([KDD 2024 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/kdd/kdd2024.json)), some of them at Cainiao Network according to search results ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3637528.3671783), snippet). An earlier version reports over 89.7 percent feasibility without a solver ([review-dataset mirror](https://github.com/weathon/split_review_2/blob/main/datasets/deepreview_13k_train/human_reviews/joMMM9eadc.md), self-reported). No deployment is reported.

Routing began with DIFUSCO from Carnegie Mellon (NeurIPS 2023 Spotlight, [NeurIPS 2023 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/nips/nips2023.json)). Junchi Yan's Thinklab at Shanghai Jiao Tong leads in volume with T2T, Fast T2T, GenSCO, UniCO and COExpander ([Fast T2T](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/Fast-T2T); [UniCO](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/UniCO)), and JKU Linz contributes unsupervised samplers, DiffUCO and SDDS ([repo](https://github.com/ml-jku/DIffUCO)). Scalability work includes DISCO, which reports inference up to 5.28 times faster than other diffusion solvers but was rejected at ICLR 2025 ([ICLR 2025 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/iclr/iclr2025.json), self-reported), and DEITSP (KDD 2025), a one-step non-autoregressive TSP model reporting 0.10 percent on TSP-100 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13767), snippet, self-reported). DIFU-Ada (AAAI 2026, arXiv 2502.12188) adapts a trained diffusion solver at inference time without retraining and transfers zero-shot from TSP to prize-collecting TSP and orienteering; search results attribute it to Haoyu Lei, Kaiwen Zhou, Yinchuan Li and Zhitang Chen of Huawei Noah's Ark Lab, with CUHK ([AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/40948), snippet).

Multi-vehicle routing remains the weak spot. Among the solvers above, plus the ICLR 2026 and ICML 2026 accepted lists, COExpander (ICML 2025) is the only diffusion-family solver found with a published head-to-head against HGS on capacitated routing. Its consistency-model predictor plus local search trails HGS by 3.90 to 5.34 percent on CVRP-50 to 500 and also trails KAIST's reinforcement-learning Sym-NCO given the same local search, which its authors attribute to "limitations of the global predictor" ([COExpander PDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/ma25r/ma25r.pdf), Table 6). Carnegie Mellon's Combinatorial Adjoint Matching (ICML 2026) ships a CVRP-100 script ([CAM](https://github.com/Shengyu-Feng/CAM)), but its CVRP results were not visible, and no diffusion result against LKH-3 on capacitated routing was found. A Diffusion-Enhanced GFlowNet for vehicle routing was rejected at ICLR 2026 ([ICLR 2026 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/iclr/iclr2026.json)). KAIST, named often in this field, works through reinforcement learning and transformers rather than diffusion: RL4CO is "an Extensive Reinforcement Learning for Combinatorial Optimization Benchmark" with KAIST authors from Jinkyoo Park's group ([NeurIPS 2023 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/nips/nips2023.json); [RL4CO](https://github.com/ai4co/rl4co)), and RouteFinder trains transformer policies ([RouteFinder](https://github.com/ai4co/routefinder)).

Scheduling has no top-venue diffusion paper. SymmetricDiffusers (ICLR 2025 Oral) learns permutation distributions but does not evaluate shop scheduling ([ICLR 2025 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/iclr/iclr2025.json)). Applied journals carry an "inverse diffusion" method for flexible job shops ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2210650225001373), snippet) and diffusion-enhanced reinforcement learning for hybrid flow shops ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417426027272), snippet), neither reporting deployment.

### Packing and layout

FamiPacking (IET, January 2024) calls itself the first diffusion system for 3D bin packing, and a summary states that "the techniques have been applied to improve the packing system of Taiwan FamilyMart" ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10461157/), snippet, self-reported); scale and permanence are unknown. DiffusionPack, shown at NeurIPS 2025 but absent from the main-track list, uses a language model to turn packing preferences into guidance without retraining ([NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/137139), snippet), and its quantitative results could not be verified. A 2026 preprint combining diffusion and reinforcement learning for online 3D bin packing ([arXiv 2604.10953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10953)) could not be opened. Earlier, MIT's Diffusion-CCSP composed per-constraint diffusion models to pack objects stably ([repo](https://github.com/zt-yang/diffusion-ccsp)).

Retail shelf layout offers the plainest statement of this report's premise. A planogram paper trains on "successful shelf arrangements across multiple retail locations" and claims, in simulation, 94.4 percent constraint satisfaction ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00527), snippet, self-reported), so a model that learned from success still violated constraints about 5.6 percent of the time.

At warehouse scale, Diffusion Co-Design (DiCoDe) from Amanda Prorok's Cambridge lab co-optimizes robot policies and warehouse layouts, with projected guidance keeping configurations within hard constraints. Rejected at ICLR 2026 and accepted at ICML 2026, it reports 39 percent higher reward in a warehouse benchmark with 66 percent less simulation than the prior state of the art, a figure the ICML abstract counts in "simulation steps" and the earlier ICLR abstract in "simulation samples" ([ICML 2026 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/icml/icml2026.json); [ICLR 2026 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/iclr/iclr2026.json), self-reported). Its arXiv version (2511.03100) dates from November 2025.

### Robot motion, picking and path finding

Warehouse robotics builds on Diffusion Policy ([repo](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy)). Carnegie Mellon's MMD (ICLR 2025 Spotlight) composes single-robot diffusion models with multi-agent search for "dozens of robots" in scenarios "motivated by logistics environments" ([ICLR 2025 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/iclr/iclr2025.json)). The University of Virginia's SMD (ICML 2025; Liang, Christopher, Koenig, Fioretto) puts constrained optimization inside sampling to produce collision-free, kinematically feasible multi-robot trajectories and reports beating classical and learned planners on a new benchmark ([ICML 2025 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/icml/icml2025.json), self-reported). Projected Coupled Diffusion (ICLR 2026) reports "guaranteed constraint satisfaction" in multi-robot planning ([ICLR 2026 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/iclr/iclr2026.json), self-reported). DiffLNS warm-starts the LNS2 path-finding solver with discrete diffusion and reports 95.8 percent success in congested settings ([arxiv-daily digest mirror](https://github.com/ycpku/arxiv-daily/blob/main/reports/2026-05-14.md), self-reported).

For picking, DiffusionVLA (ICML 2025, with Midea Group) reports 63.7 percent zero-shot bin-picking accuracy on 102 unseen objects ([ICML 2025 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/icml/icml2025.json), self-reported). No public statement was found that Covariant, Dexterity, Symbotic or Amazon Robotics use diffusion policies in production; Amazon licensed Covariant's robot foundation models in 2024 without describing their architecture ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-covariant-ai-robots), snippet). On the road, Tsinghua's Diffusion Planner (ICLR 2025 Oral) was evaluated on "our newly collected 200-hour delivery-vehicle driving dataset" ([project page source](https://github.com/ZhengYinan-AIR/zhengyinan-air.github.io/blob/HEAD/Diffusion-Planner/index.html)), and its successor was road-tested on a Xiaomi vehicle ([project page source](https://github.com/ZhengYinan-AIR/zhengyinan-air.github.io/blob/HEAD/Hyper-Diffusion-Planner/index.html)), which is not a logistics deployment.

### Synthetic data

NVIDIA's Cosmos-Transfer is a diffusion video model conditioned on segmentation, depth or edge maps; a distilled version generates "in a single diffusion step (vs. 36 steps)" ([Cosmos-Transfer1](https://github.com/nvidia-cosmos/cosmos-transfer1)), and Cosmos-Transfer2.5 documents augmenting synthetic robot-navigation data for sim-to-real transfer ([Cosmos-Transfer2.5](https://github.com/nvidia-cosmos/cosmos-transfer2.5)). NVIDIA pitches Cosmos for robot synthetic data ([NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/national-robotics-week-2026/), snippet), but its Mega warehouse digital-twin blueprint could not be opened, and product availability is not evidence of any operator's production use. Trajectory work includes DiffTraj ([code](https://github.com/Yasoz/DiffTraj)), ControlTraj's topology-constrained road trajectories and physics-guided vessel-trajectory imputation by Zhang, Fan, Lv, Song and Shibasaki, both at KDD 2024 ([KDD 2024 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/kdd/kdd2024.json)). DiffScene (AAAI 2025), from UIUC with Carnegie Mellon and Gatik AI, generates safety-critical driving scenarios ([AAAI 2025 list](https://github.com/papercopilot/paperlists/blob/main/aaai/aaai2025.json)). Synthetic data is not automatically private: Carlini and colleagues extracted training examples from image diffusion models ([USENIX Security 2023](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/carlini); [metadata mirror](https://github.com/chaosarium/CMU-LTI-RAG/blob/main/collection/papers/Daphne%20Ippolito%20%7C%20Publication%20%7C%20Extracting%20Training%20Data%20from%20Diffusion%20Models.txt)).

The strongest production evidence for a generative model trained on an operator's own history lies outside diffusion: Amazon's DeepFleet, trained on its robot fleet's movements, is reported to improve travel efficiency by 10 percent ([Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/blog/amazon-builds-first-foundation-model-for-multirobot-coordination), snippet, self-reported). Diffusion's most deployable role today is as a scenario and synthetic-data engine for conventional optimizers and as a proposal generator inside hybrid solvers; a plan that appears on its own, ready to execute, remains a research demonstration.

### Evidence gaps

arXiv, OpenReview, IEEE, AAAI, USENIX, NVIDIA and conference sites were blocked, so accepted-paper lists, GitHub files and search summaries stood in. Not verified: the FamilyMart claim, DiffusionPack's results, DIFU-Ada's affiliations and numbers, CSDI's and TSDiff's primary CRPS tables, CAM's CVRP results, the scenario-tree and online packing preprints, NVIDIA Mega, a continuous-space multi-agent path-finding projected-diffusion paper, Diff-RNTraj, a 2026 foveated diffusion policy for warehouse fulfillment, and whether WeatherNext Gen was a Cloud product. The screen misses most journals and all internal company work, so absence of public evidence does not prove absence of use.
